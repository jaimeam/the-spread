# The Spread - Thursday, 15 October 2026

Two more ships have sunk near Hormuz and Brent is back above $106. Saudi authorities have now confirmed a fire at Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and the Houthis are promising more strikes on oil sites, while the US diesel arbitrage to Europe has reopened.

## Hormuz: two ships sunk, fresh strikes on Tuesday

A Singapore-flagged containership sank after a projectile hit it in the strait. A dry bulk carrier also went down, weeks after an earlier deadly strike [gCaptain]. One tally cited by OilPrice counts 12 vessels attacked between 5 and 11 October. It adds that a tanker and a container ship were hit by unknown projectiles on Tuesday, 13 October [OilPrice.com]. The Tuesday strikes are new events on a new date, so the attacks are continuing beyond Sunday's six-ship day.

OilPrice attributes the campaign to Iran. The Tuesday projectiles are described as unknown. Separately, US Central Command says it destroyed an Iranian-affiliated dhow in the Gulf of Oman on 6 October that was helping target US warships [CENTCOM via gCaptain].

The attacks are undermining a recent recovery in Gulf oil shipments, and traders are no longer responding to peace talk from Washington. Brent is back over $106 [OilPrice.com], with rising shipping costs part of the lift [Rigzone].

The supply gap is large. As of Kpler's 6 October note, about 5 Mb/d of Middle East crude was still offline and more than 2bn bbl had been lost since the war began. Saudi loadings hit 9.1 Mb/d in late September, a post-COVID high, and Kpler expects about 9 Mb/d in October [Kpler]. UAE output rose 400,000 b/d to a record 5.3 Mb/d in September as flows through Hormuz were restored [IEA via World Oil]. That was before the latest escalation.

Japan's cabinet approved three bills on 13 October. They subsidise oil imported from outside Hormuz, support tanker insurance and finance alternative transport infrastructure [Splash247]. Possible knock-on: subsidised Japanese buying could pull more Atlantic Basin crude east.

## Aramco HQ: Riyadh confirms a fire, Houthis threaten more

Saudi authorities have now reported a limited fire at Aramco's Dhahran office complex after Tuesday night's missile attack [OilPrice.com]. Sources told Reuters the damage was extensive [gCaptain]. A source familiar with the matter says oil production was not affected [Rigzone].

The Houthis also claim strikes on an Aramco refinery in Riyadh and on King Khalid airport. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed those. The group says it will hit Saudi airports and oil facilities again in the coming days and has told workers to evacuate them [OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. With no output lost, the effect so far is risk premium rather than barrels.

Possible knock-on: strikes on export infrastructure would put at risk the East-West pipeline flows that Kpler says have recovered.

## US diesel: arb to Europe reopens as Washington invokes the DPA

Over one week, the November USGC-to-NWE diesel arbitrage swung from about -$18/t to +$6.75/t [Kpler]. NWE cash differentials firmed and MR freight fell from 375WS to 310WS [Kpler]. Rising LR rates keep the India-to-Med lane shut [Kpler]. Europe's prompt diesel pull therefore lands on the US Gulf rather than India.

At home, Vice President Vance said on Tuesday the administration has used the Defense Production Act to expand Delek's refinery in Tyler, Texas. US diesel prices are above $6 [OilPrice.com]. No capacity figure was given.

The API estimates US crude stocks fell 11.12m bbl in the week to 9 October, after 2.09m bbl the week before [API via OilPrice.com]. EIA figures for that week are not in today's data. The latest EIA week, to 2 October, showed a 3,186 kb crude draw and distillate stocks of 105,138 kb [EIA].

## Gas: storage gains slow, Gulf LNG still stranded

EU storage stood at 74.0% on 13 October [GIE AGSI]. Daily gains have narrowed over three readings, from +0.14 to +0.10 to +0.08 points [GIE AGSI].

Qatar's shipments had all but stopped by late August, after a projectile hit one of its tankers outside the strait. Doha is now trying older tankers and new tactics [gCaptain].

In South Asia, high spot prices are destroying demand. India's gas demand is down 1.5m t so far this year. Before the war, Wood Mackenzie had expected South Asian LNG demand to grow by 2.7m t (7%) [Wood Mackenzie via OilPrice.com].

Trump says Taiwan will buy 30% of the planned Alaska LNG project's output for 20 years, worth $70-80bn, plus a $4.4bn equity stake. Taipei says no binding commitments have been finalised [gCaptain; OilPrice.com].

## Russia: Moscow backs the diesel deal, strikes Ukraine's grid

Deputy PM Novak called the US diesel agreement a positive signal for Russia's economy and tied it to higher Russian refining and output. Finance Minister Siluanov said on Tuesday that Russia levies no export duty on diesel [OilPrice.com].

Separately, Russia says it carried out a massive overnight strike on Ukrainian power plants [Rigzone]. That sits awkwardly with the energy truce Trump announced, which neither Moscow nor Kyiv has confirmed.
