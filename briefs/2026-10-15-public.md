# The Spread - Thursday, 15 October 2026

Two ships have sunk near the Strait of Hormuz and Brent is back above $106, as attacks keep undoing the partial recovery in Gulf loadings. Saudi Arabia now acknowledges a fire at Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and the Houthis say more strikes on oil sites are coming.

## Hormuz: container ship and bulker sink

A Singapore-flagged container ship sank after being struck by a projectile in the strait. A dry bulk carrier hit in an earlier strike has also gone down [gCaptain]. One tally counts 12 vessels attacked between 5 and 11 October, including the six on Sunday 11 October. A tanker and a container ship were struck on Tuesday 13 October [OilPrice.com], so attacks are continuing on new dates. US forces also destroyed an Iranian-linked dhow in the Gulf of Oman on 6 October, saying it was helping to target US warships [CENTCOM via gCaptain].

Brent is back above $106 [OilPrice.com]. OilPrice.com argues that White House talk of peace no longer moves prices the way it did earlier in the war.

The supply hole is large. In its 6 October note, Kpler put Middle East crude losses at more than 2bn bbl, with about 5 Mb/d still offline [Kpler]. Saudi loadings hit a post-COVID high of 9.1 Mb/d in late September as East-West pipeline flows recovered and underground storage began to unwind. Kpler expects Saudi supply to average about 9 Mb/d in October [Kpler]. The IEA says UAE output rose 400,000 b/d to a record 5.3 MMbpd in September [IEA via World Oil].

Buyers are paying to route around the strait. Japan's cabinet approved three bills on 13 October to subsidise the extra cost of oil from outside Hormuz, back tanker insurance, and fund alternative transport infrastructure [Splash247].

Today's reports do not show how much the October attacks have cut loadings since September's highs. October tanker-tracking data will settle it.

## Houthis threaten more Saudi oil strikes after Aramco HQ fire

Saudi authorities now report a limited fire at Aramco's Dhahran office complex from Tuesday night's missile attack [OilPrice.com]. Sources told Reuters the damage was extensive [gCaptain]. A source told Rigzone that oil production was not affected [Rigzone].

The Houthis also claim to have hit an Aramco refinery in Riyadh and King Khalid airport. Neither claim is confirmed. They say attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities will resume in the coming days, and have told workers to evacuate [OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. Possible knock-on: if the threats land on export sites, they would hit the Saudi supply that is currently offsetting losses inside the Gulf.

## US diesel: Europe arb reopens, White House reaches for DPA

Cheaper medium-range tanker freight has reopened the US-to-Europe diesel trade. In the week to 13 October, MR rates fell from 375WS to 310WS while Northwest Europe cash differentials firmed [Kpler]. As a result, November US Gulf Coast to Northwest Europe diesel arbs swung from about -$18/t to +$6.75/t [Kpler]. That pulls Gulf Coast barrels toward Europe.

The India-to-Mediterranean route stays shut because larger LR tanker rates are rising [Kpler]. Europe is therefore leaning on the US Gulf rather than East-of-Suez supply.

At home, US diesel prices remain above $6 [OilPrice.com]. Vice President Vance said the administration used the Defense Production Act to expand Delek's Tyler, Texas refinery. No capacity figure was given [OilPrice.com].

The API estimates US crude stocks fell 11.12m bbl in the week to 9 October [API via OilPrice.com]. EIA figures for that week are not yet out. The latest EIA data, for the week to 2 October, show commercial crude at 424,134 kb (-3,186 kb) and distillate at 105,138 kb [EIA].

## Gas: South Asian demand destruction, storage creeps up

The Gulf LNG squeeze is now cutting demand, not just raising prices. Wood Mackenzie says India's gas demand is down 1.5m t so far this year as spot prices rose [WoodMac via OilPrice.com]. Before the war it had expected South Asian LNG demand to grow 2.7m t, or 7%, this year.

Qatar is trying to restart flows with older tankers and new tactics. Its shipments had all but stopped by late August after a projectile hit one of its tankers outside the strait [gCaptain].

EU storage was 74.0% full on 13 October, up 0.08 points [GIE AGSI].

Trump says Taiwan will buy 30% of Alaska LNG's output for 20 years. Taipei says no binding commitments have been finalised [gCaptain; OilPrice.com].

## Russia: Moscow backs diesel deal, strikes Ukrainian power plants

Novak called the US diesel agreement a positive signal for Russia's economy. He linked it to higher Russian refining and production [OilPrice.com]. This is Moscow's first on-record endorsement of the diesel deal.

Separately, Russia says it carried out a massive overnight strike on Ukrainian power plants [Russia via Rigzone]. That sits awkwardly with the energy truce announced only by Trump.
