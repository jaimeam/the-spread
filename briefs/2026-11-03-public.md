# The Spread - Tuesday, 3 November 2026

Crude lost nearly 7% on Monday after Washington and Tehran both signalled room for diplomacy, though tankers were still being hit in the Gulf. In Europe, Germany entered winter well short of its gas storage obligation, with TTF above €100/MWh.

## Brent sinks 7% on Iran talks

Brent traded at $105.60/bbl on Monday, down $7.85, and WTI fell $7.20 to $96.92 [OilPrice.com]. That removed part of the war premium built up through October. The slide began in Asian trade, with Brent at $109.82 early Monday [OilPrice.com]. It deepened after a Reuters source said a draft proposal to end the US-Iran war was circulating once Iranian negotiators had left Qatar [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

Tehran's account is more guarded. It says contacts with the US continue, but that days of talks with Qatari mediators produced altered proposals and no breakthrough [gCaptain]. President Pezeshkian said the Supreme Leader had given the government some authority over peace talks [Iranian state media via OilPrice.com]. The existence of a draft rests on one unnamed official, and neither government has confirmed its terms.

The drop now runs against the banks' forecasts. Goldman raised its December Brent forecast to $105 from $90, citing supply-security risk [Goldman Sachs via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Monday's close was already about at that level. ADNOC's Sultan Al Jaber, opening ADIPEC, said nearly 1bn bbl has been drawn from global inventories since the Hormuz disruption began. He also said logistics now make up more than 30% of a barrel's cost, and that diesel stocks in some markets are at seasonal lows [World Oil; OilPrice.com]. That leaves a market selling off a risk premium while physical buffers stay thin.

The draft's terms and any formal US response aren't in today's reports. Whether attacks on Gulf shipping stop will show if the premium stays out.

## Germany misses gas fill goal

Germany filled its storage to about 61%, against the EU obligation of 80% by the start of winter [OilPrice.com]. The European benchmark has stayed above €100/MWh since topping that level last week [OilPrice.com]. EU-wide storage was 74.6% full on 1 November, down 0.06 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

For the region's largest consumer, entering the heating season this far below target leaves it reliant on prompt LNG at a time when Qatari supply is disrupted. That keeps TTF exposed to any cold snap. As a possible knock-on, sustained gas above €100 raises refinery hydrogen and fuel costs and pressures European ammonia and petchem runs.

Separately, the WSJ reports that Trump envoys Kushner and Witkoff discussed with Moscow restoring Russian pipeline gas to Europe through Ukraine, sold via a US intermediary [WSJ via OilPrice.com]. The report rests on unnamed sources, and no party has confirmed talks. EU law would be a significant obstacle.

## Attacks reach the northern Gulf

The weekend's incidents came despite the diplomacy. A tanker sailing southeast through the Gulf was struck by two unidentified projectiles late Sunday [gCaptain; Splash247]. UKMTO separately reported that an Emirati-owned offshore tug was hit by two projectiles about 83 miles east of Ras al-Khafji, near the Saudi-Kuwaiti border [UKMTO via OilPrice.com]. That location is hundreds of miles from the strait. OilPrice attributes the strike to Iran, but none of the items cites an official confirmation of the attacker.

Gulf producers are building routes around the strait. Iraq has raised exports via Ceyhan to 500,000 b/d from about 350,000 b/d by trucking Basrah crude north to the pipeline system [North Oil Company source via Kurdistan 24 via OilPrice.com]. That figure comes from a single source. The UAE energy minister said the country will invest in new export routes, storage abroad and shipping fleets [gCaptain].

## Ukraine resumes refinery strikes

Ukraine has hit three Russian refineries since Friday, resuming its campaign late last week [OilPrice.com]. Satellite fire data analysed by Bloomberg shows a fresh heat anomaly that may be a fire at the Samara crude pumping station in the Volga region [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Damage and the affected capacity are not reported. More idled distillation capacity would further cut product exports at a time when Russia has already extended its producer diesel export ban.
