# The Spread - Tuesday, 27 October 2026

Yemen's Houthis claim strikes on pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West crude pipeline, the main route for Gulf crude that avoids Hormuz, and Egypt has voted to help defend the kingdom. Oil eased from a six-month high as regional security chiefs flew to Tehran. Indian refiners are turning to Gulf barrels and away from Russia.

## Houthis claim pipeline hits

The Houthis say they hit pumping stations on the East-West pipeline and Riyadh's King Khalid airport on Monday. Neither claim is confirmed [OilPrice.com]. The same day, Egypt's parliament authorised military support to defend Saudi Arabia, despite a Houthi warning to Cairo last week [OilPrice.com].

The pipeline matters because it carries crude across the kingdom to Yanbu on the Red Sea, the export route that bypasses Hormuz. Any outage there would push Saudi barrels back toward a strait still under attack. Egypt joining the conflict could also put Red Sea and Suez traffic more directly in the Houthis' sights; that knock-on is possible, not reported.

There is no Saudi or Aramco statement on the pumping stations in today's reports. A ministry statement or Yanbu loading data would show whether flows were touched.

Sources: [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Egypt-Votes-to-Defend-Saudi-Arabia-Risking-Houthi-Fire.html)

## India trims Russian crude

BPCL will buy less Russian crude in November, its finance head said on Monday. High freight costs and premiums on Russian grades now make Middle East offers cheaper [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone reports a wider shift by Indian refiners toward Persian Gulf crude [Rigzone].

The driver is freight. Long-haul Russian voyages have become expensive, while shorter Gulf trips win on delivered cost. That is consistent with 22 October data showing the Oman-Dubai premium at its lowest since end-July [OilPrice.com, JODI]. If the shift spreads, Russian sellers lose their main buyer, which would widen Urals discounts. That effect is possible, not yet reported.

Sources: [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Indias-BPCL-to-Cut-Russian-Oil-Imports-Due-to-Higher-Prices.html) · [Rigzone](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/gulf_oil_bargains_lure_india_away_from_russia-26-oct-2026-184931-article/?rss=true)

## Tanker disabled in the strait, October a record

On Monday, a tanker was confirmed disabled by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz. A second vessel was reported on fire in the Persian Gulf [gCaptain]. October is now the worst month on record for attacks on merchant ships in and around the strait [gCaptain].

The beach closures in Ras Al Khaimah now have a stated source. Satellite images show the spill comes from a damaged tanker and is widening, and more UAE beaches have closed [gCaptain].

Mercuria has accused the Baltic Exchange of publishing a distorted benchmark tanker rate to avoid losing business to a rival [gCaptain]. That matters for charter settlements priced off the benchmark.

Oil slipped from its six-month high as security officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan headed to Tehran to revive talks [Rigzone]. No price level is given in today's reports.

## Russian diesel output climbs for the US deal

Two industry sources say Russian refiners have raised diesel output since export quotas under the US supply deal arrived in mid-October [Reuters via OilPrice.com]:

- **September:** about 175,000 t/d.
- **First half of October:** about 160,000 t/d.
- **Since the quotas:** climbing toward 180,000 t/d.

Domestic demand typically drops to about 140,000 t/d in October, which leaves room for exports [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

Separately, a bipartisan group of US lawmakers has warned banks and port authorities against handling these cargoes [gCaptain]. That puts pressure on the financing and discharge of the tanker due in Norfolk.

## LNG importers pay more for less; EU storage resumes injections

Rystad estimates LNG importers will spend at least $39bn more in 2026 while buying 60.7m t less [Rystad via OilPrice.com]. Lost Qatari and UAE volumes are pushing buyers into the spot market at higher prices.

EU gas storage broke a week-long plateau:

- **24 October:** 74.6% full, flat since 18 October [GIE AGSI].
- **25 October:** 74.9% full, up 0.21 points [GIE AGSI].

A UBS-hosted call noted European gas futures nearing €90/MWh earlier in the week. The meteorologist on the call suggested a strong El Niño could soften the winter squeeze [OilPrice.com].

## Gulf sulphur still constrained

Hormuz sulphur exports are running at about 55% of pre-war pace [Kpler]. Buyers in China, Chile, Morocco, Indonesia and Africa's copper belt are affected unevenly. The squeeze reaches sulphuric acid supply for metals leaching and fertiliser.

Sources: [Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/how-kpler-can-help-track-sulphur-supply-chain-associated-market-impacts)

## EQT to buy Range

EQT will acquire Range Resources in an all-stock deal valued above $50bn including debt. The combined company will produce more than 9 Bcfe/d of Appalachian gas [OilPrice.com; World Oil]. Range holders get 0.987 EQT shares each, a 20.6% premium [OilPrice.com].
