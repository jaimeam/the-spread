# The Spread - Wednesday, 25 November 2026

Four more ships were attacked around Hormuz, and a marine insurer now advises owners against non-essential transits, yet Brent sits near three-month lows. Iraq is disputing its capacity assessment ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting, and European gas storage fell at its fastest pace this month.

## Four more ships hit in Hormuz

Four more commercial vessels were attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz, and tugboats are now among the targets [gCaptain]. That brings gCaptain's running count of reported tanker strikes to 10 [gCaptain]. gCaptain's headline frames the attacks as Iran widening its crackdown. Separately, Iran claims three tankers struck mines in the strait [Iranian claim via OilPrice.com]; that claim has not been independently confirmed.

The shipping side is reacting. The Swedish Club, a marine insurer, now advises owners against non-essential transits, citing attacks, congestion and navigational interference [gCaptain]. Fewer owners willing to transit means demand concentrates on the tonnage still available. That premium shows up in asset prices: 15-year-old VLCCs are now worth considerably more than newbuilds [gCaptain]. Gas carriers are tight too. VLGC earnings on the Ras Tanura–Chiba benchmark are close to $250,000/day, enough to pull ethane carriers into LPG trades [Baltic Exchange via Splash247].

The US is also investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on up to 50 tankers [WSJ via gCaptain]. That is a reported probe, with no findings yet.

Crude has barely priced the attacks. Brent was $88.67/bbl and WTI $79.59 early Tuesday, both near three-month lows [OilPrice.com]. Oil then closed higher as the attacks overshadowed diplomacy, though no figure is given [Rigzone].

The items don't name the ships, their cargoes or the damage for the four new attacks. UKMTO incident notices will show whether any loaded crude or LNG carriers were disabled.

## Iraq contests OPEC+ capacity

Baghdad rejects an OPEC+ assessment that it can sustainably produce less than 5m b/d. It wants about 6m b/d recognised before Sunday's meeting, where 2027 targets are expected to be discussed [OilPrice.com]. Iraq's current limit is 4.431m b/d [OilPrice.com]. A higher capacity baseline would give Iraq a case for more quota headroom in a market that forecasters already see tipping into surplus.

Saudi Arabia, meanwhile, now expects its economy to shrink 2.1% this year rather than 3.6% [Saudi budget statement via OilPrice.com]. Its October exports were the highest since the war began, with Aramco moving more crude through the East-West pipeline to Yanbu [OilPrice.com].

Baghdad is also seeking federal control of North Oil Company fields in Kurdistan after its investigation found smuggling there [Iraqi oil minister via INA via OilPrice.com].

## EU storage draw quickens as TTF rises again

EU storage stood at 68.9% on 22 November and 68.4% on 23 November, a 0.50-point daily draw [GIE AGSI]. TTF front-month topped €90.50/MWh early Tuesday, its fourth straight session of gains, as a cold snap set in [OilPrice.com].

In its October report, the GECF put TTF up 12% month on month and 163% year on year [GECF via OilPrice.com].

Asia is now competing harder for the same cargoes. Japan's physical spark spread has turned positive on a seven-day average for the first time since the war began, so gas-fired power is profitable again and utilities may buy more spot LNG [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: more Japanese spot buying would compete with Europe for Atlantic cargoes during the storage draw.

Longer term, Equinor agreed an 18-year deal to supply VNG in Germany from 2027 to 2044 [Offshore Energy].

## Crude cheap, diesel still tight

Brent has fallen more than 20% in November, but product markets, diesel especially, remain tight despite recent declines in margins [OilPrice.com]. US retail diesel fell below $6/gal last week [OilPrice.com].

Cheap crude is drawing buyers. Chinese state and independent refiners have increased purchases this month to refill stocks [Rigzone]. South Korea says it has secured November–December crude at 90% of last year's levels and January at 75% [Korean trade ministry via OilPrice.com].

API estimates US crude stocks rose 126,000 bbl in the week to 20 November [API via OilPrice.com].

## Russia shipping pressure widens

Zelenskiy said Ukraine carried out a drone strike on a Russian cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland [gCaptain]; Russia's response isn't reported. Ukraine's foreign minister called the weekend strike in Libyan waters self-defence, after criticism from Tripoli [gCaptain].

Brussels' proposed 22nd sanctions package would ban tankers insured in Russia [European Commission via Splash247]. It is a proposal only, not yet adopted.
