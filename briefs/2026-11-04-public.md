# The Spread - Wednesday, 4 November 2026

Dubai crude has slipped into contango for the first time since July as Gulf barrels pile up, even as the world stays short of diesel. Brent whipsawed on conflicting US-Iran peace headlines after Washington confirmed a strike on Qeshm Island.

## Dubai flips to contango

The November Dubai contract has moved into contango for the first time since early July. That signals Middle East crude supply now exceeds demand [OilPrice.com]. The Dubai M1-M3 spread was near zero this week [OilPrice.com].

The cause is volume. Gulf producers have stepped up shuttle shipping through Hormuz, transferring cargoes ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman once tankers clear the strait. Physical flows have risen gradually for two months [OilPrice.com].

The Dubai cash-to-futures spread is routinely used to set Middle Eastern official selling prices (OSPs), so a flatter curve feeds into what Asian buyers pay for Gulf grades [OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: softer December-loading OSPs for medium-sour crude.

Cheaper freight is also reopening long-haul arbitrages. BPCL bought 1m bbl of WTI for late December to early January delivery to Mumbai, at about $18/bbl over Dated Brent, as freight eased from early-October records [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

## Diesel stays short to 2027

The crude glut has not reached products. Asian crude imports are back at pre-war levels, but imports of gasoline and especially diesel from the Middle East have collapsed because of reduced refining capacity there [OilPrice.com]. OilPrice expects the diesel crunch to last well into 2027.

The global picture shows a partial recovery:
- **Q2 2026:** global refinery inputs fell 4.8m b/d, or 6% [Kpler via EIA].
- **Q3 2026:** throughput recovered 1.8m b/d as refiners adapted to new trade flows [Kpler via EIA].

Fuel oil is tight too. Singapore VLSFO bunkers topped $1,000/t in October, the first time since March [Ship & Bunker via Splash247]. In the US, distillate stocks were 102,824 kb in the week to 23 October, down 1,462 kb [EIA].

## US confirms Qeshm strike as Brent whipsaws

US officials confirmed a strike on Iran's Qeshm Island, near Hormuz, on 2 November. They described it as one of a series of previously undisclosed limited strikes [WSJ via OilPrice.com].

Brent moved sharply on the competing headlines:
- **Monday:** closed at $104.77 after a 7.6% fall [OilPrice.com].
- **Overnight:** rebounded on the strike report [OilPrice.com].
- **Tuesday morning:** fell from $107.24 to $100.80 on conflicting peace-talk reports, then recovered to around $104 [OilPrice.com].

The diplomatic signals remain one-sided. A diplomat briefed on the matter says both sides are reviewing new proposals from mediators [Rigzone]. Tehran dismissed the reports of momentum [OilPrice.com]. Neither government has confirmed the terms or the status of the talks.

Rystad sees a limited deal possible by mid-2027, with Hormuz flows unlikely to return to pre-war levels [Rystad via OilPrice.com].

## IEA sees gas tight into 2027

The IEA says Europe enters the heating season with its lowest gas storage in over a decade, and expects the global market to stay tight well into next year [IEA via OilPrice.com]. EU storage was 74.3% full on 2 November, down 0.24 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

US cargoes are filling part of the gap:
- **To Europe:** 6.89m t in October, up 1m t [LSEG via Reuters via OilPrice.com].
- **To Asia:** 2.73m t, against 2.84m t in September [LSEG via Reuters via OilPrice.com].

Egypt shows the strain on importers. OilPrice reports that QatarEnergy extended its force majeure on 28 October, which earlier only a buyer had reported. With that supply gone, Egypt's LNG bill has doubled. Cairo is deferring cargoes into 2027 and burning fuel oil in power plants [OilPrice.com]. Its own gas output fell to 3.63 bcf/d in August [OilPrice.com].

On new supply, ADNOC Gas will supply TotalEnergies with up to 1.6m t of LNG over five years, starting as early as this year [OilPrice.com].

## Aramco profit up despite attacks

Aramco's third-quarter adjusted net income was $31.2bn, against $28.6bn a year earlier. That beat the $28.7bn analyst consensus [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Higher prices and refining earnings offset attacks on the East-West pipeline and other assets [OilPrice.com; Rigzone].

A separate analysis gives headline net income of $36.25bn, up 34.5%. It notes that exceptional gains lifted the figure while physical operations deteriorated [OilPrice.com].
