## 1. Hurricane Isaias: Gulf Coast landfall window has passed, damage picture not yet in our items

**What changed.** EIA reports that Hurricane Isaias, a Category 2 storm [EIA], was forecast by NOAA to make landfall on the Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday. That forecast was as of the morning of 9 October [EIA]. The headline says offshore production shut-ins are under way. The summary gives no volumes, no affected operators and no refinery names.

EIA describes it as the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season [EIA]. It also calls it the largest storm system to reach the region since Hurricane Helene in September 2024 [EIA].

**Timing caveat.** The piece carries a 12 October publication stamp, but its forecast data is from 9 October. Landfall should therefore have happened over the weekend (10-11 October). Our items do not say where it came ashore, at what strength, or whether infrastructure was hit. Shut-ins are confirmed by EIA as an agency source. Everything below about refineries, ports and LNG is conditional on the landfall location, which we do not have.

**Transmission chain**

- **Offshore crude.** Landfall forces evacuations and shut-ins on Gulf of Mexico platforms. US crude supply dips briefly, and Gulf-produced medium-sour grades are hit hardest. Restarts usually follow within days if platforms are undamaged.
  - *Who feels it:* USGC sour-crude buyers in the short term. The effect is mostly offset if refineries are also cutting runs.
- **Refining (map: us_gulf).** If the storm tracked into the Texas/Louisiana refining belt, plants may have cut runs as a precaution or lost power. Flooding of process units is the bigger risk.
  - *Mechanism:* The USGC is Europe's swing diesel supplier, so lower runs plus port closures mean fewer export barrels.
  - *Who feels it:* NWE diesel cracks. Latin American importers who rely on USGC gasoline and diesel also lose barrels.
  - *Domestic angle:* Northeast buyers compete with Europe for the same USGC barrels. That supports the NY Harbor diesel premium.
- **Ports and terminals (link not on map).** Port closures halt crude and product exports even where refineries keep running.
  - *Effect:* Cargo bunching after reopening and tighter prompt tanker availability on transatlantic product routes.
  - *Who feels it:* European buyers face a short gap, then a wave of arrivals.
- **LNG (link not on map, conditional).** Most US LNG export capacity sits on the Texas/Louisiana coast.
  - *Mechanism:* Shutdowns, lower feedgas or channel closures delay cargoes.
  - *Who feels it:* European gas (map: europe_gas). Prompt TTF matters more going into winter.
  - *Status:* No item today confirms an LNG impact. Flagged as a channel to watch, not a reported event.
- **Petchem (link not on map, conditional).** USGC ethane crackers and derivative units often shut ahead of storms. Restarting crackers is slow and often brings flaring and off-spec periods.
  - *Who feels it:* Polyethylene and other derivative export availability, and the domestic ethane balance.

**How to read the next updates.** The key question is whether this was a shut-in-and-restart storm or a damage storm.

- **Shut-in-and-restart:** Offshore output and refinery runs normalise within roughly a week. The net effect on crude is small, with at most a brief diesel-crack bump.
- **Damage storm:** Flooded refineries, power loss or terminal damage would turn this into a weeks-long product story felt mainly in European diesel.

Until operator statements or agency shut-in tallies appear in our items, we are not assigning numbers.

## Signals I'm not sure about

- **Stale-looking timing.** The EIA item is stamped 12 October but carries 9 October forecast data. Either it was republished, or the published date in our feed is off. Do not read it as a fresh warning of a landfall still to come.
- **The Helene comparison.** EIA compares storm-system size, not expected energy damage. A large Category 2 can be disruptive or benign depending on track. Do not infer the size of the energy impact from it.
- **A first hurricane in October.** That is late for the season. It may say something about this year's activity, but one storm is not a pattern. Keep an eye on any follow-on systems in the Gulf before the season ends.
