# The Spread - Monday, 12 October 2026

**Top line:** The Gulf tanker war widened over the weekend and crude rose. Brent hit $106.80, up $2.09 [OilPrice.com], and WTI hit $93.78, up $1.93 [OilPrice.com]. Early Monday the Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire" had pulled prices lower, but the Middle East attacks outweighed it by the US session. European gas is at its highest since early 2023.

## Gulf: attacks spread beyond the Strait

**What changed:**
- **Tankers off Oman:** A drone struck the tanker Petro Dolphin at Sohar Anchorage, off Oman, on Sunday. Two Indian seafarers are missing.
- **Tankers in the Strait:** Another tanker was hit by a projectile while exiting Hormuz. gCaptain reports two more tankers struck inside the Strait.
- **US blockade:** US forces disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman. Washington says the ship ignored warnings while trying to run its blockade of Iran.
- **Saudi Arabia:** The Houthis hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh with ballistic and cruise missiles on Saturday.
- **Talks:** Iran's security chief called the US reply to Iran's seven conditions for reopening Hormuz "insufficient". There is no US comment in the items.

**Transmission:** Strikes now reach anchorages outside the Strait. That adds to Gulf loading and transit risk, and the risk premium is showing up in crude benchmarks. Freight is already stretched: Clarksons' ClarkSea index rose 12% in a week to a record $84,951/day [Clarksons via Splash247]. Possible knock-on: higher war-risk costs widen delivered-price gaps for medium-sour buyers in Asia.

**Saudi position (claims, not verified flows):**
- At the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh, Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz said the kingdom's export capability is intact despite the attacks. No item gives export data to test this.
- He also said Riyadh is looking to expand pipeline capacity to outlets outside the Gulf to 10 mb/d [Saudi Energy Ministry via gCaptain], within two to three years. That is a long-dated hedge, not near-term relief.

**Outlook views:**
- BMI expects Brent to stay above $100 for months [BMI via OilPrice.com]. It sees a US-Iran deal by end-Q1 2027 at the earliest.
- Standard Chartered warns the market is mistaking adaptation for normalisation.

**Diesel:** The IEA says Hormuz is straining global diesel markets in particular and is coordinating with governments. France is releasing strategic reserves. TotalEnergies will cut French diesel and heating-oil prices further from 1 November.

## Russia: Trump's "energy ceasefire" and diesel deal

**The claim:** Trump said Sunday that Russia and Ukraine agreed to an energy ceasefire, effective immediately. **Neither Moscow nor Kyiv confirms it in today's items.** Rigzone separately reports Zelenskyy is on a collision course with Trump.

**The diesel deal is under attack in Washington:**
- Republican Senator Thom Tillis said Trump's move to ease restrictions on Russian diesel may be illegal.
- Republican Representative Brian Fitzpatrick plans to force a House vote on a bill blocking Russian oil purchases.

**Context:** Russian product exports reached a three-month high in September [Rigzone].

**Transmission:** If the truce holds, fewer strikes on Russian refineries would mean more exportable Russian diesel. That is bearish for diesel cracks, but with a lag while plants are repaired. For now the truce is one-sided and contested, and Monday's price action showed Gulf risk dominating.

**Black Sea freight:** Lukoil's Bulgaria manager says Black Sea oil-tanker insurance has risen from about $200,000 to $2.5 million per vessel [Lukoil Bulgaria via OilPrice.com]. That is a single-source claim.

## European gas: TTF near €83

**Price:** Dutch TTF trades around €83/MWh, the highest since early 2023 [OilPrice.com]. The September dip to about €70 [OilPrice.com] did not last.

**Why:** Europe faces winter with no Qatari LNG exports and storage well below 15-year lows. EU storage stood at 73.7% full on 11 October, up 0.13 pts day on day [GIE AGSI]. Gas Infrastructure Europe says winter flexibility is adequate but margins are tighter.

**Transmission:** Lost Qatari LNG plus thin storage leaves Europe dependent on LNG imports, which lifts TTF. Possible knock-on: higher refinery fuel and hydrogen costs, and some gas-to-oil switching that supports diesel.
