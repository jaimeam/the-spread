# The Spread - Thursday, 5 November 2026

Crude fell about 9% on Wednesday after the White House tied its midterm losses to the Iran war and Trump said he had no immediate plans to strike Iran. Gas stayed tight: a fire took Norway's Visund export capacity offline, and US distillate stocks drew sharply.

## Oil crashes 9% after midterms

Brent fell $8.94 (8.6%) to $94.66/bbl by early Wednesday afternoon in New York, touching its lowest level since 4 September. WTI fell $8.01 to $88.08 [OilPrice.com]. Brent traded near $118 last Friday [OilPrice.com]. The session was volatile. Brent was up early, then stood at $100.60 by mid-morning, and Murban slipped $1.27 to $100.90 [OilPrice.com].

Democrats took both chambers of Congress [Saxo Bank via Rigzone; gCaptain]. Trump told the Financial Times there were no immediate plans to strike Iran, and he put the defeat down to gasoline prices [OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. Vance went further and blamed the eight-month war itself for the losses [gCaptain].

Traders have priced a settlement, and the risk premium came out of prompt crude. A settlement is still only priced, not agreed: neither Washington nor Tehran has published terms. gCaptain argues the defeat may make an exit harder, not easier. Gulf flows have also slipped from recent highs [OilPrice.com], and two consultancies, Rystad among them, warned against counting on a quick supply rebound [OilPrice.com].

The physical risk hasn't gone away. The IRGC says four tankers hit naval mines south of Hormuz, in an area it had declared closed, and are still burning. It also says two other vessels were seized [IRGC via OilPrice.com]. Only Iran has said this. No owner, flag state or UKMTO report confirms it.

## Fire halts Visund gas output

Equinor shut its Visund platform in the Norwegian North Sea on Wednesday after a fire in an electrical switchboard room. All 13.2m cubic metres a day of the field's gas export capacity is off the market from Thursday [NRK via OilPrice.com]. No restart date is reported.

The outage cuts supply into a system that is already drawing. EU storage was 74.1% full on 3 November, down 0.26 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. ECMWF data show early signs that the polar vortex is breaking down, which could send cold air into Northwest Europe [OilPrice.com]. That is a forecast signal, not yet weather.

The midterm news briefly pulled TTF down: front-month fell 2% early Wednesday [OilPrice.com]. In Asia, the JKM benchmark topped $31/MMBtu this month and briefly passed $33, against a little over $9 in January [OilPrice.com].

## US distillates draw 3.6m bbl

US distillate stocks fell 3,565 kb to 99,259 kb in the week to 30 October [EIA]. That is the second weekly fall after 102,824 kb in the week to 23 October [EIA]. The draw came even though refinery utilisation rose 1.7 points to 94.5% [EIA], so higher runs are not keeping up with offtake.

Other stocks also fell. Commercial crude dropped 865 kb to 409,760 kb, which is 4% below the five-year average [EIA; OilPrice.com]. Gasoline slipped 222 kb [EIA].

Cheaper crude and tighter diesel together point to wider distillate cracks. Possibly, that adds pull on US Gulf Coast barrels from the Northeast, at the expense of Europe.

The emergency Jones Act waiver expires on 15 November. The American Maritime Partnership is urging Trump to let it lapse [gCaptain].

## Butane hits 30-year high

Northwest European November butane reached $1,125/t in early November, its highest in 30 years and above the 2022 peaks [OilPrice.com]. Asia's AFEI butane index was $1,180/t on 3 November, and Mont Belvieu reached $920/t, a 14-year high [OilPrice.com]. The main driver is the reshaping of propane and butane trade flows since the war began in March [OilPrice.com]. Cracker operators that run LPG as feedstock face higher costs.

## UAE weighs third Hormuz bypass

ADNOC is weighing a third crude export pipeline that would avoid the strait. The UAE already runs one bypass line and is building a second [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. The report rests on people familiar with the matter, and ADNOC has not confirmed it.

Separately, Aramco has started crude production from its Zuluf offshore expansion, which is designed for up to 600,000 b/d [Splash247]. Zuluf is in the Gulf, so its barrels still have to exit through Hormuz.
