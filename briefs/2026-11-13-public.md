# The Spread - Friday, 13 November 2026

Trump said the US no longer needs a deal with Iran, and Brent briefly jumped back to $94 after trading near $91 earlier in the day. US inventories built across crude, gasoline and distillates, and the transatlantic diesel arbitrage is shut for every December arrival.

## Trump says US needs no deal

On Thursday, Trump posted that the US has no need for a deal with Iran. On Monday he had suggested the war was nearing its end. He also claimed Washington controls Iran's money and the Strait of Hormuz; that is his assertion, and Tehran has not responded in today's items. Brent briefly hit $94 after the post [OilPrice.com]. Earlier in Asian trade, on peace hopes for both Iran and Ukraine, it had been $91.32, with WTI at $85.32 [OilPrice.com]. Crude is about 20% below the late-October peak of $118 [OilPrice.com].

The Ukraine track gave little to price in. Talks in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November agreed in principle on a prisoner exchange but made no progress towards a ceasefire [OilPrice.com].

A projectile struck the engine room of the Liberia-flagged crude tanker Dominica Prosperity off the UAE on Wednesday. All 22 crew were evacuated [gCaptain]. Oman is also managing a tanker that ran aground off Al Qibliyah Island and the handling of its cargo [gCaptain].

The swing in crude is reaching shipping costs. Bunker prices fell by up to 20% in under two weeks [Splash247].

## US distillates up 4.1m bbl

US distillate stocks reversed in the week to 6 November:

| | Week to 30 Oct | Week to 6 Nov |
|---|---|---|
| Distillate stocks | 99,259 kb, down 3,565 kb [EIA] | 103,330 kb, up 4,071 kb [EIA] |

Other figures for the week to 6 November:
- Commercial crude rose 4,657 kb to 414,417 kb [EIA], 4% below the five-year average [EIA via OilPrice.com].
- Gasoline rose 1,799 kb [EIA].
- Total commercial petroleum stocks rose 11.4m bbl [EIA via OilPrice.com].
- Refinery utilisation reached 95.2% [EIA].

Europe's pull on those barrels has weakened. Weaker Northwest Europe cash differentials have shut the transatlantic MR arbitrage for every December arrival [Kpler]. Late-December MR margins fell $5.25/t to -$3.25/t [Kpler]. A 4% drop in forward freight, to around 385 WS, would reopen the arbitrage [Kpler]. Until then, the Gulf Coast's swing diesel is not heading to Europe, and higher US distillate stocks are building at home.

The EIA also raised its 2026 and 2027 Brent forecasts in its latest Short-Term Energy Outlook; the figures are not given in the item [EIA via Rigzone].

## India buys more Gulf crude than Russian

So far in November, Indian imports of crude from the Gulf reached about 2.1m b/d, against 1.8m b/d from Russia [Kpler via Rigzone]. It is the first time Gulf barrels have led since the war began. The shift reflects recovering Hormuz flows and Russian crude losing its price advantage [Kpler via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

Gulf grades are cheapening as they compete for buyers:
- The Dubai M1-M3 spread collapsed from $11.03 to $1.03/bbl [Kpler].
- Gulf producers have cut official selling prices [Kpler].
- Prompt Agbami cargoes into Ningbo are about $4/bbl more competitive than Murban [Kpler].

Other Asian buyers are looking elsewhere. Unnamed traders and shipbrokers say Sinopec and SK Energy have bought African and Brazilian grades as long-haul freight eases [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. Separately, ASEAN agreed a joint crude stockpile, with storage in Indonesia and Thailand [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

Possible knock-on: pressure on Russian discounts to win back Indian demand.

## EU storage slides to 71.2%

EU gas storage was 71.2% full on 11 November, down 0.35 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. Germany was at 58% in the previous update [GIE via OilPrice.com]. Asian spot LNG is above $30/MMBtu [OilPrice.com]. Even so, Rystad's winter outlook does not call a further rally and lays out as many downside paths as upside ones [Rystad via OilPrice.com].

Europe still relies on Russian LNG ahead of the January ban. The EU paid an estimated €8.7bn for Yamal LNG in January-October, already more than in all of 2025 [Urgewald/Kpler via OilPrice.com; gCaptain].

## Exxon sanctions $12.5bn Longtail

ExxonMobil took a $12.5bn final investment decision on Longtail, its eighth Stabroek development and its first gas-condensate project offshore Guyana [World Oil]. It is planned to process 1.2 Bcfd of gas and produce up to 250,000 b/d of condensate from 2030 [World Oil].

In other supply news, Nigeria pumped 1.51m b/d of crude in October, at its OPEC quota for a sixth straight month [NUPRC via OilPrice.com].
