# The Spread - Friday, 16 October 2026

The IEA is rushing more than 20 million barrels of diesel to market, and Washington is weighing relief for shadow-fleet tankers to cool record freight. Ukraine's strike on Russia's Ryazan refinery ended Kyiv's pause on energy targets, so diesel remains the tightest part of the barrel.

## IEA rushes diesel, US pushes for more

The IEA will release more than 20m bbl of diesel as soon as possible and speed up the rest of its draw. Total releases are now set to exceed the 400m bbl pledged in March. About 330m bbl has gone out so far and roughly 100m bbl is still to be delivered [IEA via OilPrice.com].

Separately, the US is informally pressing IEA members to release at least 100m bbl more, according to unnamed sources [World Oil]. The IEA has not confirmed that.

Releasing diesel goes straight at the product shortage, unlike crude releases. OilPrice.com's analysis argues the shortage comes from lost refining in the Middle East and Russia, which the rest of the world cannot offset, even as Middle East crude flows approach pre-war levels [OilPrice.com]. Aramco's CEO called the global crude squeeze serious [Rigzone].

Possible knock-on: prompt diesel cracks could ease if the barrels arrive quickly. No timing per country is given.

## Shadow-fleet relief floated as freight soars

The Trump administration is considering easing sanctions on shadow-fleet vessels that have carried oil and products [Reuters via gCaptain]. Nothing has been decided. Bringing that tonnage back into compliant trade would loosen the tanker pool. Investors moved first: COSCO Shipping Energy fell more than 13% in Hong Kong as Asian tanker stocks sold off [Splash247].

Freight is the pressure behind the idea. A VLCC voyage from the US to Japan in late November will cost $81m, against $9.1m on average last year [gCaptain].

Allies are moving the other way. The UK sanctioned two more Russian energy companies and a dozen shadow-fleet tankers [gCaptain]. OFAC issued new Iran-related designations and amended general licences on 15 October; details are not in the summary [OFAC].

On prices, Brent for December traded at $106 (-0.2%) and November WTI at $98 (+3.6%) on Thursday afternoon [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone ties the divergence partly to shifting shipping costs [Rigzone].

## Ryazan hit as Kyiv ends energy pause

Ukrainian drones struck Rosneft's Ryazan refinery on Thursday. The governor confirmed a fire [OilPrice.com]. The plant handles nearly 5% of Russian refinery throughput [OilPrice.com].

The strike followed Zelenskyy's statement that Moscow had ignored the energy ceasefire promoted by Trump. That truce was only ever announced from Washington.

Lost Ryazan runs mean less Russian product available for export, which bears on diesel. The scale of the damage isn't reported.

Standard Chartered argues the US-Russia diesel arrangement would not add new supply [OilPrice.com]. Within the EU, Bulgaria's prime minister broke ranks to back the deal, citing high fuel prices [OilPrice.com].

## US stocks: big crude and distillate draws

In the week to 9 October [EIA]:

- **Commercial crude:** fell 8,557 kb to 415,577 kb. That is still 3% above the five-year average [EIA via OilPrice.com]. API had reported an 11.12m bbl draw [API via OilPrice.com].
- **Distillate:** fell 2,527 kb to 102,611 kb.
- **Gasoline:** rose 3,518 kb to 208,262 kb.

The crude draw drove Thursday's WTI rally [OilPrice.com]. Refinery utilisation for the week isn't in today's data.

## Hormuz: CENTCOM claims control

US Central Command says the US and regional partners control the Strait of Hormuz. It cites more than 40 commercial ships transiting in the past 24 hours [CENTCOM via gCaptain]. The claim comes from one side only. gCaptain reports ship attacks at a new high but gives no count in the summary.

Fresh detail on this week's strikes:

- The sunken feeder GFS Pride was struck on Tuesday [Splash247].
- All 21 crew of the sunken Singapore-flagged vessel were evacuated without injury [Rigzone].

On LNG, Pakistan LNG's chief warned of winter gas shortages because transit of Qatari and UAE cargoes through the strait remains heavily disrupted [PLL via OilPrice.com].

## Black Sea: chemical tankers hit off Romania

Two chemical tankers caught fire just outside Romanian territorial waters overnight, and seven crew are missing. Romanian officials say Russian drones strayed into national airspace [gCaptain].

Separately, the Yara J reported a suspected drone strike while anchored inside Romanian waters off Sulina. The vessel was in ballast and the fire was put out [Splash247].
