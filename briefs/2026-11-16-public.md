# The Spread - Monday, 16 November 2026

The IEA says the oil market has tipped into surplus for the first time since the Iran war began, as Gulf exports recover. Hormuz traffic is still about two-thirds below normal, and Iran claims two more ships hit mines in the strait.

## IEA flags first war surplus

The IEA's monthly report on Friday said global supply now exceeds demand for the first time since the war started. It expects a fourth-quarter surplus because Middle East exports are recovering faster than demand, despite continued attacks on shipping [IEA via Rigzone; IEA via Reuters via gCaptain]. The report does not give the size of the surplus.

Iraq drove much of the recovery. October crude output reached 4.6m b/d, up 540,000 b/d from September and the highest since 2019 [IEA via OilPrice.com]. Kuwait and Saudi Arabia also raised October output. The barrels leave Hormuz on smaller tankers, which then transfer cargoes to VLCCs in the Gulf of Oman. That keeps exports moving while large-ship transits stay thin.

Asian buyers are responding. Chinese, Japanese and Thai refiners are buying more Gulf crude, including cargoes that must transit the strait, because freight has come off record highs and producers are offering discounts to regional benchmarks [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. That account rests on unnamed traders. Possible knock-on: Asian refiners buying more Gulf barrels may draw less on Atlantic Basin crude.

Prices did not follow the bearish balance. Brent settled near $96 on Friday [Rigzone], up from $91.32 early Thursday [OilPrice.com], on attacks on Saudi infrastructure and continued threats to shipping. December WTI still lost $2.63 on the week, or 2.9%, after trading between $83.88 and $90.83 [OilPrice.com]. That was crude's second straight weekly fall.

## Two mine hits claimed

Iran says two ships hit mines in the strait on consecutive days, but neither strike has been independently confirmed:
- **Friday:** the IRGC navy said an LNG carrier struck a mine and suffered an explosion while using what it called an unauthorised route [IRGC via gCaptain].
- **Saturday:** Iran's navy said an oil tanker caught fire after hitting a mine in the eastern strait [Iranian state media via Reuters via gCaptain].

Reuters could not verify the Saturday report. No vessel names, owners or UKMTO reports appear in today's items.

Shipping remains the bottleneck even as exports recover. Vessel traffic through Hormuz is about 65% below pre-conflict levels [gCaptain]. Asian jet fuel prices are about 75% above pre-war levels, and airlines whose hedges are expiring must decide whether to lock in at current prices [industry executives via Reuters via OilPrice.com].

## Jones Act waiver runs to February

Washington extended the Jones Act waiver by 90 days on Friday, the third extension of a waiver first issued in March. A White House official confirmed it to Reuters, and a US Customs and Border Protection notice dated 13 November set it out [OilPrice.com; CBP via Splash247]. Foreign-flagged tankers can keep moving oil between US ports into February. Each voyage still needs individual approval under a restriction added in August. This keeps more ships available to carry Gulf Coast barrels to other US coasts through winter.

## Ninth Russian refinery hit in November

Ukraine has now struck nine Russian refineries since the start of November [Rigzone], up from six by 9 November. Damage and run losses are not reported.

The pressure extends beyond refineries:
- **Black Sea:** on Sunday, drones struck three commercial ships near Romania's Sulina canal, according to Romania's prime minister, and fighter jets were scrambled [Romanian government via Reuters via gCaptain].
- **Sanctions:** Britain sanctioned 11 ships tied to Russian energy exports (five LNG carriers, five oil tankers and a floating storage unit) plus nine companies, including Slavneft and Rosneft's Vostok Oil project [Splash247].
- **Revenue:** despite the strikes, Russia's October crude and product export revenue was the highest since May, lifted by war-driven prices [IEA via gCaptain].

## Tehran plans for zero oil revenue

A senior Iranian budget official told the government to draft next year's budget on the assumption that oil revenue could fall to zero [OilPrice.com].
- **Loadings:** Iran has loaded no crude since 25 August [US Treasury via OilPrice.com].
- **Oil at sea:** cargoes held outside the US blockade fell from 88.8m bbl in mid-July to zero by mid-October [OilPrice.com].
- **Revenue timing:** Chinese buyers pay one to two months after delivery, so Kpler expects revenue to stop soon [Kpler via OilPrice.com].

Separately, a restricted IAEA report says Iran is still refusing inspectors access to verify its enriched uranium stockpile [IAEA via OilPrice.com].

## EU storage at 70.5%

EU gas storage was 70.5% full on 14 November, down 0.21 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. EU power prices topped €600/MWh last week during a wind drought [OilPrice.com], which raises reliance on gas-fired generation as storage draws.

Asian buyers are also short. Pakistan LNG Limited has reissued a tender for two cargoes, for delivery on 14-16 December and 9-11 January at Port Qasim, after rejecting earlier bids [Business Recorder via OilPrice.com].
