# The Spread - Thursday, 29 October 2026

Qatar has extended its LNG force majeure to the end of the year, so Europe and Asia head into winter without Qatari term cargoes. The first Russian diesel cargo bound for the US has dropped New York as its destination after lawmakers threatened legal action.

## Qatar LNG halt to year-end

QatarEnergy has told Italy's Edison that the force majeure in place since April now runs until the end of December [Edison via OilPrice.com; LNG Industry]. The confirmation comes from a buyer. No QatarEnergy statement or affected volumes appear in the items.

With term cargoes missing for the rest of the year, buyers have to compete for spot LNG. European gas rose to its highest level since autumn 2022 [OilPrice.com]. India shows the pressure in Asia. Qatar supplied about 45% of its 24.9m t of LNG imports in 2025, and India is now heading for record October imports at prices that have tripled in a year [OilPrice.com]. EU storage stood at 75.0% full on 27 October, up 0.03 pts d/d [GIE AGSI], so stocks are still building only slowly.

Possible knock-on: dearer gas raises refinery fuel and hydrogen costs and could support switching from gas to oil products, which would firm diesel and fuel oil.

## Diesel tanker for NY idles

The Malta-flagged Agioi Fanendes left Primorsk on 22 October with about 40,000 t of ULSD and was due in New York on 7 November [OilPrice.com]. Lloyd's List had it idling off Skagen on 26 October. Its tracking now reads "for orders", meaning no declared port [Lloyd's List via OilPrice.com].

The change follows a letter from Senator Blumenthal on 27 October. He told the owner that delivering the cargo would breach federal law and asked for the ship to turn around [gCaptain]. A bipartisan group of lawmakers has also warned US banks and port authorities that handling Russian diesel could expose them to civil and criminal liability [OilPrice.com]. That legal risk now falls on anyone who would receive or finance the cargo.

Moscow tells a different story. Novak says three cargoes are underway and a fourth was due to sail on 28 October [Novak via Splash247]. The US has not confirmed this. Separately, a UBS note puts Russian oil exports only about 10% below pre-war levels [UBS via OilPrice.com].

## US warns ships off needless Hormuz runs

The US military has told vessels with no need to move between Gulf of Oman and Hormuz ports to stay away [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. At least 50 tankers are stuck in the Gulf [OilPrice.com].

Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority has now set a start date for its threat against Sinokor, the largest VLCC owner. Sweeping action against Sinokor's fleet begins on 1 November, it says [Splash247], and Rigzone describes it as "legal action". This is Iran's claim only, and no measure has been taken yet.

Oil rose on Wednesday on a report of an Iran attack. No price figure was given [Rigzone]. ING says traders are positioning for renewed hostilities after the 3 November midterms, and expects prices to drop if no escalation follows [ING via OilPrice.com]. On the diplomatic side, Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani delegations met Iranian officials in Tehran on Monday. Tehran wants US sanctions and the port blockade addressed before broader talks [OilPrice.com].

## China pays up for Atlantic crude as Iranian barrels vanish

As of a 21 October note, Iranian oil on water outside the blockade zone had fallen to zero, and Kpler sees Iran's oil revenue on course to vanish by mid-December [Kpler]. With those barrels gone and competition for Russian crude stronger, Chinese refiners are buying West African and Brazilian grades for December delivery. They are paying premiums of up to $20/bbl over ICE Brent, helped by freight easing over the past two weeks [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

Formerly sanctioned tankers are joining the Gulf trade. Two VLCCs that once carried Venezuelan crude are now lifting Gulf oil, and two more are heading to the Gulf of Oman [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

## Gulf flow reads diverge; UBS lifts Brent

Ship-tracking data show Saudi exports holding near wartime highs this month despite the attacks [Rigzone]. Basrah exports are rebounding toward 3.00 Mb/d in October [Kpler]. UBS takes the other side, saying Gulf flows remain below prewar levels. It raised its end-2026 Brent forecast to $110 from $100 and its 2027 curve to $95 for March, $90 for June and $85 for September [UBS via OilPrice.com].

Freight earnings remain extreme. Scorpio's LR2s are booking an average of $181,000/day [Scorpio via gCaptain], and Bahri's Q3 net profit rose tenfold to $1.36bn [Bahri via OilPrice.com].

## US crude stocks fall below five-year average

In the week to 23 October, commercial crude fell 1,892 kb to 410,625 kb [EIA]. That puts stocks 4% below the five-year average, against 3% above in the week to 9 October [EIA via OilPrice.com]. Distillate stocks fell 1,462 kb to 102,824 kb, and gasoline rose 3,870 kb to 209,180 kb. Refinery utilisation slipped 0.6 pts to 92.8% [EIA].
