# The Spread - Wednesday, 14 October 2026

A Houthi missile attack reportedly hit Saudi Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and Riyadh struck back at Sanaa, taking the Saudi-Houthi war into the kingdom's oil heartland. In the Gulf, empty supertankers are steering away from a key transfer hub while Japanese buyers return to load crude by ship-to-ship transfer outside Hormuz.

## Houthi strike hits Aramco's Dhahran headquarters

Aramco's Dhahran headquarters was hit during a Houthi missile attack and caught fire, according to unnamed sources [World Oil]. Aramco and Riyadh have not confirmed the hit in today's items. The report frames it as part of escalating strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure. On Tuesday, Saudi forces struck the Yemeni capital Sanaa, according to the Houthis, after the Saudi-led coalition warned civilians to stay away from an oil depot on the city's outskirts [gCaptain].

Dhahran sits on the Gulf coast, away from the Red Sea bypass at Yanbu, so the threat now reaches both of Saudi Arabia's export coasts. The reports don't say whether any production, processing or export facility was damaged. Aramco statements or loading data from Ras Tanura and Yanbu will show that. Brent was hovering near $105 [OilPrice.com], and prices settled mixed on Tuesday as traders took profit [Rigzone].

Separately, Saudi Arabia told OPEC its crude output rose by a third last month, recovering from a three-decade low [Rigzone]. That is Riyadh's own figure, and no volumes are given in the item.

## Gulf loading: VLCCs turn away, Japan returns via STS

A growing number of empty supertankers are turning away from the Persian Gulf as Iranian attacks threaten a critical transfer hub for wartime oil shipments [gCaptain]. At the same time, Japan-owned or Japan-bound tankers are returning to the region for the first time since the war began in February. None are transiting Hormuz. They are loading through ship-to-ship transfers off Oman and Fujairah in the Gulf of Oman [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; Rigzone].

Gulf crude is reaching Asian refiners through transfers outside the strait, and each cargo ties up extra tonnage. That feeds record tanker rates. The freight squeeze is now tipping the Americas heavy-crude market into oversupply while Asia and Europe run short of heavy grades [OilPrice.com]. Canada produces over 5m b/d, mostly heavy, and about 4m b/d of it goes to US refineries [OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: wider heavy-crude discounts for US Gulf Coast refiners.

## EU storage gains slow

EU gas storage stood at 73.9% full on 12 October, up 0.10 points on the day, after a 0.14-point gain on 11 October [GIE AGSI]. Middle East LNG has not recovered in any meaningful way after seven and a half months of war. Before the war, about a fifth of global LNG passed through Hormuz [OilPrice.com]. Equinor signed another supply agreement with Eneco for Norwegian gas, its third with Eneco this year for the Netherlands and Germany [Rigzone].

## Black Sea: ship sunk off Bulgaria, 11 missing

A drone attack sank a cargo ship off Bulgaria, leaving 11 sailors missing. It is possibly the second deadly sinking in the area in a week, and two ships were hit in total [gCaptain]. Bulgaria summoned Russia's ambassador and points to Russia, an attribution Moscow has not accepted in the items. Separately, France is taking a suspected Russian shadow-fleet tanker to Réunion, which Moscow calls piracy [gCaptain]. Sweden released the EU-sanctioned product tanker Sea Owl I after nearly seven months, once it was confirmed as legitimately registered in Equatorial Guinea [Splash247].

## Demand side: China imports recover, OPEC goes big on 2027

Chinese refiners will slow drawdowns from crude reserves this month as imports recover [Energy Aspects via Rigzone]. That adds Chinese bids for seaborne crude in a tight tanker market.

OPEC forecasts 2027 demand growth of 2.6m b/d, after 200,000 b/d this year. That takes world demand from 105.7m b/d to 108.3m b/d, partly on inventory rebuilding [OPEC via OilPrice.com]. This is a forecast, not a flow.
