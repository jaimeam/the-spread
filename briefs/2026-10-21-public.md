# The Spread - Wednesday, 21 October 2026

Iranian strikes in the Gulf are doing heavier damage to ships, and VLCC spot rates have passed $1m a day, so producers are cutting prices to keep buyers. In Europe, a sharp drop in Norwegian gas output and a near-stall in storage injections tighten an already thin winter balance.

## Hormuz: engine-room hits and $1m-a-day VLCCs

Iranian attacks in the Persian Gulf are hitting more ships and doing more severe damage than earlier in the conflict, with engine rooms increasingly targeted [gCaptain]. A ship with a wrecked engine room is out of service far longer than one with hull or deck damage. That removes tonnage from an already short fleet.

Freight shows the strain. VLCC spot rates now exceed $1m per day [OilPrice.com]. The same report says producers are cutting crude prices so buyers can absorb the extra transport cost, which shifts the hit from refiners' delivered costs onto producers' realisations.

The IRGC also claimed on Tuesday that a supertanker caught fire after striking a mine south of the strait. This is the latest in a run of such claims, and no independent evidence has surfaced [gCaptain]. Neither report gives a fresh tally or names the ships hit since last week. Independent confirmation from navies or owners would settle both points.

## Crude eases on Iran talk headlines; Asian LNG hits a 2022 high

Oil fell back from five-month highs on Tuesday as uncertain Iran negotiations drove volatile trading [Rigzone]. Brent was cited at $108, with the Brent-WTI spread at $5 [OilPrice.com].

Unnamed senior officials say Tehran is deliberately dragging out talks to weaken Trump before November's midterms [gCaptain]. This comes from anonymous sourcing, and Tehran has not said so publicly. If it holds, any easing of the Gulf risk premium or relief on Iranian barrels is further off.

In gas, Asian LNG rose above $30/MMBtu on JKM, its highest since December 2022 [OilPrice.com]. The drivers are repeated tanker attacks around the Middle East and Europe's worsening winter outlook. China and Japan are buying 8-10% less LNG year on year [OilPrice.com]. That demand restraint has not been enough to cap prices.

## Europe gas: Norway output falls 12.6%, storage barely moves

Norwegian gas output fell 12.6% in September from August [Norwegian Offshore Directorate via OilPrice.com]. Total petroleum production averaged about 3.77m boe/d, down 10.4% month on month and 4% below the directorate's forecast [Norwegian Offshore Directorate via OilPrice.com]. The reports give no cause for the drop. Norway is Europe's largest pipeline supplier, so a shortfall there has to be made up with LNG that is competing with Asia at multi-year highs.

EU storage shows the squeeze. Injections nearly stalled: storage was 74.6% full on 19 October, up 0.01 points day on day, against +0.11 points on 18 October [GIE AGSI].

Equinor separately scaled back electrification at Troll, Norway's biggest gas field, saying the original plan would have cost production at the wrong time [Rigzone]. Eni began start-up at Meleiha Phase 2 in Egypt, designed for up to 100 MMcfd [World Oil]. Possible knock-on: more Egyptian gas could trim Egypt's LNG imports and leave more cargoes for Europe.

## Shadow-fleet plan takes shape: sell, refurbish, re-flag into legal trade

The plan now has a mechanism. Energy Secretary Wright said Washington is authorising the sale of sanctioned tankers to approved investors, who would refurbish them and return them to legitimate crude trades to ease freight costs [Splash247]. This is still Wright's account, and no Treasury terms or vessel numbers are published.

Bloomberg analysis suggests the plan may draw only a sliver of the dark fleet [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. It cites existing contracts, complex compliance checks and high asset prices as obstacles. On that reading, any relief to tanker rates would be modest and slow.

Sources: [Splash247](https://splash247.com/us-moves-to-legitimise-shadow-tanker-fleet/) · [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/trumps-plan-to-revive-sanctioned-tankers-faces-tough-sell/)

## Russia: seaborne crude exports at lowest weekly level since March

Shipments from the Baltic port of Primorsk stopped for several days. Weekly Russian seaborne crude exports fell by 390,000 b/d to 3.13m b/d, the lowest since March [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. The four-week average to 18 October was 3.59m b/d, down 110,000 b/d. Loadings were 31 tankers carrying 21.91m bbl, against 34 carrying 24.63m bbl the week before [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. The report does not say why Primorsk stopped. Fewer Baltic barrels mean less Urals for Indian and Turkish buyers and lower export revenue for Moscow.

Sources: [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Russias-Oil-Export-Revenue-Slides-as-Production-Declines.html)

## US: API shows another crude draw; Citgo cashes in on margins

The API estimated a 4.93m bbl crude draw in the week to 16 October [API via OilPrice.com]. The API puts commercial crude down more than 56m bbl over 27 weeks, with SPR draws keeping total US crude near the start-of-year level. The EIA weekly for the same week, due today, is not in today's reports.

Citgo raised its 2026 operating profit outlook by $2bn on surging fuel margins, according to a document seen by Bloomberg [Bloomberg via Rigzone]. That is direct evidence that Gulf Coast refiners are capturing the product tightness.
