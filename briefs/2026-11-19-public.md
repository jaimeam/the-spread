# The Spread - Thursday, 19 November 2026

Washington widened its Iran blockade to cover ship-to-ship transfers, and another Gulf tanker attack left a crew member missing. Crude still fell about 3% as US stocks built across crude, gasoline and distillates.

## CENTCOM widens Iran blockade

US Central Command issued an advisory on Wednesday. Ships that take cargo at sea from vessels loaded in Iran will now be treated as trying to evade the US blockade of Iranian ports, and could face action [CENTCOM via gCaptain]. Until now enforcement covered the Iranian ports themselves. This targets the transshipment step that hides a cargo's origin. Any tanker taking part in such transfers now carries blockade risk, and so do the buyers further down the chain, mainly Chinese, if the cargoes reach them. The advisory doesn't say how many cargoes it would catch; the first enforcement actions CENTCOM reports will show its reach.

The strikes are also going both ways. US officials say American aircraft hit an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday [US military official via Rigzone]. The IRGC says it had first attacked a US military site in Bahrain [IRGC via OilPrice.com]. That claim comes only from Iran, and no damage is reported. Gulf states issued fresh warnings to residents about incoming projectiles [OilPrice.com].

## Crude falls 3% despite attacks

Oil fell about 3% on Wednesday, with traders citing rising supply, a stronger dollar and weaker risk appetite [Rigzone]. Early Wednesday, Brent traded at $92.49/bbl and WTI at $84.73 [OilPrice.com].

The EIA weekly added to the bearish tone. In the week to 13 November:

- **Crude:** commercial stocks rose 3,380 kb to 417,797 kb [EIA], 3% below the five-year average [EIA via OilPrice.com].
- **Gasoline:** stocks rose 5,192 kb [EIA].
- **Distillates:** stocks rose 1,571 kb to 104,901 kb [EIA], a second straight weekly build.
- **Refinery runs:** utilisation was 95.7%, up 0.5 points [EIA].
- **Total:** commercial petroleum stocks rose 9.2m bbl [EIA via OilPrice.com].

## ADNOC vessel hit, crew member missing

ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels was attacked while transiting Hormuz on the evening of 17 November. One crew member is unaccounted for, 11 were injured and the rest were evacuated [ADNOC L&S via OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. The company has not named the vessel or the attacker.

The LPG carrier TARA GAS was still burning and adrift off Musandam on Wednesday, according to a navigational warning [gCaptain]. India's foreign ministry confirmed that 24 of its 25 crew are accounted for [Indian MEA via Splash247].

With supertankers being targeted, more crude is moving on smaller ships, mostly through ship-to-ship transfers. Suezmaxes and Aframaxes carried 2m b/d out of the strait in October, the most since the war began [Vortexa via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Each of these ships carries less than half a VLCC load, so the same export volume ties up more hulls and more transfers.

## Diesel carries refining margins

Kpler estimates that Ukrainian drone strikes keep about 3.9m b/d of Russian refining offline [Kpler, 15 Nov note]. That is well above the 2.2m b/d nameplate figure in earlier Reuters reporting. Kpler also expects Russian diesel exports to fall short of the 2 Mt plan. With less Russian gasoil on the water, gasoil is leading a distillate-driven margin rally while gasoline and naphtha lag [Kpler].

The futures positioning points the other way. In a 10 November note, Kpler said trend-following funds cut Brent exposure from 45% long to 9% long in one day, and their gasoil and RBOB positions turned net short for the first time in over four months [Kpler]. Possible knock-on: if Russian export shortfalls persist, physical gasoil tightness and short fund positions may pull further apart.

## More US LNG heads to Europe

More US LNG cargoes have diverted toward Europe in recent days, adding supply to a tight market and pressuring prices [gCaptain]. EU storage was 70.0% full on 17 November, down 0.20 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

Equinor, Europe's largest pipeline gas supplier, is fighting a cyberattack on some IT systems. The company says production is unaffected and it has no sign that data was stolen [Equinor via Offshore Energy].

In the UK, Cornwall Insight expects household energy bills to rise 26% from January 2027, the highest since 2023 [Cornwall Insight via OilPrice.com]. In the US, Lower 48 gas storage ended the injection season at 3,810 Bcf, almost 2% above the five-year average [EIA].
