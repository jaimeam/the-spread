# The Spread - Tuesday, 20 October 2026

Iran's main oil terminals have stood empty for weeks under a US naval blockade, while Iraq says its exports are back near pre-war levels. Washington also says it will let sanctioned dark-fleet tankers be sold back into legitimate trade as tanker earnings hit records.

## Iran's terminals idle as Iraq nears pre-war exports

Iran's main oil-loading terminals have had no ships for weeks. This is a sign the US naval blockade is choking Tehran's exports [gCaptain]. Next door, Iraq's oil minister told state news agency INA that October exports have averaged more than 3.4m b/d so far [Iraqi oil ministry via OilPrice.com]. That is close to the 3.3-3.5m b/d shipped before the war [OilPrice.com], and about 750,000 b/d above September's 2.65m b/d. Of September's volume, 250,000 b/d went through the Iraq-Turkey pipeline to Ceyhan [Iraqi oil ministry via OilPrice.com].

The Iraqi figure is the ministry's own and has not been independently checked. October tanker-tracking totals will test it. Taken at face value, Gulf medium-sour supply is shifting from Iranian to Iraqi barrels rather than simply shrinking. Possible knock-on: if this holds, sour-heavy refiners in India and Asia could see some easing in medium-sour availability.

## US to let dark-fleet tankers shed sanctions

Last week Washington was only weighing shadow-fleet relief. Energy Secretary Chris Wright now says the administration is authorising the sale of sanctioned tankers so they can start carrying crude legitimately [gCaptain]. Treasury terms and vessel numbers are not in the reports, and the account comes from Wright alone.

The move would enlarge the compliant tanker pool at a moment when broker Hartland estimates independent owners are earning about $400m a day above their original expectations [Hartland via Splash247]. Possible knock-on: if enough tonnage changes hands, crude freight could ease from those extremes.

Kpler separately reports that rising Hormuz attacks are hampering the growing Gulf LPG shuttle trade. Only the headline is available, with no figures [Kpler].

## Brent stalls just under $110

Brent briefly touched $109.99 overnight, then eased through Asian and European trading as signs of recovering Middle East supply offset fresh attacks [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone ties the cap to technical resistance [Rigzone].

On Friday, CENTCOM confirmed that an IRGC projectile had sunk a Singapore-flagged container ship in the strait. That day December Brent rose 3.33% to $109.50, and November WTI traded at $101.30, its first time above $100 in a month [OilPrice.com].

Wright told ABC that US gasoline and diesel prices peaked a few weeks ago. That is his view, not a data release [OilPrice.com]. The EIA's latest short-term outlook trimmed its forecast of the 2026 supply crunch, but the items give no numbers [EIA via Rigzone].

## Russia: Graham sanctions waived, diesel deal defended

Late on 16 October, Trump signed an executive order waiving the new Russia sanctions required under the Graham legislation. Kyiv and some US lawmakers have criticised the move [OilPrice.com]. Trump says both the waiver and the diesel export deal are meant to keep Putin at the peace talks [Rigzone].

An OilPrice.com commentary describes the deal as Russia releasing 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US. Neither government has confirmed that figure in today's items [OilPrice.com].

Separately, traders and data show Russian crude shipped to Asia via the Northern Sea Route reached about 20m bbl from July to mid-October, nearly 50% above last year's full season [gCaptain].

## Qatari LNG takes the Red Sea to Europe

A Qatari LNG carrier is heading north through the Red Sea toward European buyers. This is a rare transit that could shorten delivery times, but it exposes the cargo to renewed Houthi attack risk [gCaptain]. If more cargoes follow, Europe's winter supply would partly depend on the Bab el-Mandeb route.

EU storage stood at 74.6% full on 18 October, up 0.11 points day on day [GIE AGSI].

Sources: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/qatar-lng-cargo-makes-rare-red-sea-journey-to-get-gas-to-europe/)

## Hormuz knock-ons: sulfur and importers

Before the war, about 24% of world sulfur supply passed through Hormuz. Middle East sulfur has risen from about $500/t to above $800/t [OilPrice.com], which squeezes fertiliser makers who rely on refinery-byproduct sulfur.

In Pakistan, growth slowed to 2.0% in the April-June quarter as Qatari LNG supply became irregular [Pakistan Bureau of Statistics via OilPrice.com]. Canada's September inflation reached 3.1%, with fuel oil prices up 57.8% year on year [Statistics Canada via OilPrice.com].
