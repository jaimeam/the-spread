# The Spread - Wednesday, 18 November 2026

Russia's seaborne crude exports jumped to their highest since June as drone damage pushes barrels away from its refineries. US jets struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm, while Brent kept sliding on glut fears.

## Moscow's crude loadings jump

Russia loaded 30.27m bbl on 39 tankers in the week to 15 November, up from 23.6m bbl on 31 ships the week before. That is 4.32m b/d, a rise of 950,000 b/d and the highest weekly rate since June. The four-week average reached 3.9m b/d [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

Ukrainian drone strikes have knocked out domestic refining capacity, so crude that would have been run at home is going to sea. That adds tanker demand and puts more Russian crude in front of the buyers still taking it. Ukraine reported strikes on two more refineries deep inside Russia on Tuesday; damage is not reported [Rigzone]. This is Ukraine's claim only.

The EU's latest shadow-fleet package, adopted Monday, adds 12 individuals and 101 entities, including companies in the UAE and China. It now reaches oil buyers as well as owners, managers and insurers [Splash247]. A possible knock-on is wider Urals discounts, if more barrels must clear through a shrinking pool of willing ships and buyers.

## Jets strike Iran's Qeshm Island

American warplanes hit an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday. The source is a US military official speaking to Bloomberg [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. No Iranian account is in today's items, and damage isn't reported.

Separately, Iran targeted US bases in Bahrain [OilPrice.com]. Iran's and Oman's foreign ministers met in Muscat on Tuesday. Oman's account says they discussed keeping maritime corridors secure and reviving channels for negotiation, with no concrete arrangement announced [Oman Foreign Ministry via OilPrice.com]. Since 7 November, CENTCOM's new Task Force Iron Gate has been working to choke Iran's land-border and Caspian supply routes, widening pressure beyond the naval blockade [OilPrice.com].

## LPG carrier hit twice; fewer empty tankers heading in

The Panama-flagged LPG tanker TARA GAS came under repeated attack in the strait. One Indian seafarer is missing, two crew are in hospital, and the 25-member crew was forced off the ship [gCaptain].

Fewer empty tankers are now sailing to load Middle East crude, according to Tankertrackers.com. Without ballast tonnage arriving, the recent recovery in Gulf flows can't be sustained. The tracker says this puts the revival at risk [Tankertrackers via gCaptain].

## Glut talk outweighs attacks; North Sea crude heads east

Brent traded at $91.70/bbl and WTI at $85.03 early Tuesday, both below Monday's close. Glut reports outweighed the tanker attacks [OilPrice.com], though prices swung during the day [Rigzone].

API estimates US crude stocks rose 5.3m bbl in the week to 13 November, after a 9m bbl build the week before. API also reports a substantial gasoline build [API via OilPrice.com]. The latest EIA data, for the week to 6 November, show commercial crude at 414,417 kb and distillates at 103,330 kb [EIA]. EIA sees US crude output averaging 13.99m b/d in Q4 [EIA via Rigzone].

Weak Atlantic Basin crude is now finding Asian homes. Forties differentials have fallen to levels not seen since 2020, and Suezmax freight has fallen too, so the arbitrage to Ningbo has turned positive. Vitol chartered the Front Classic to carry about 1m bbl of Forties to China [Kpler].

## TTF rebounds on cold forecast

Front-month TTF rose 2% to €86/MWh early Tuesday after a 7% drop on Monday. The drivers were the Hormuz attacks, the strikes on Bahrain and forecasts of a cold spell over much of Europe from later this week [OilPrice.com]. EU storage was 70.2% full on 16 November, down 0.24 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

With more than two-thirds of Qatari LNG flows still stifled, Europe is outbidding Asia for cargoes. That lifts prices in both regions and curbs Asian demand [OilPrice.com]. In Germany, coal and lignite units hold 29 of the top 30 power-plant margins [Kpler]. The country's fourth floating LNG terminal has entered operation, completing its federal programme [Offshore Energy].

## Second gas carrier lost off Bulgaria

An LPG tanker sank off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast on Tuesday, a few dozen miles from Monday's drone-attack sinking [Bulgarian defence ministry via gCaptain]. Separately, all 19 crew of StealthGas's Liberia-flagged Gas Husky were airlifted off after a suspected drone attack. It was the second serious incident in the western Black Sea in under 24 hours [Splash247].
