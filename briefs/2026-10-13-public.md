# The Spread - Tuesday, 13 October 2026

A fuller count shows six merchant ships were hit around Hormuz on 11 October, and Gulf LNG flows remain far below pre-war levels as winter demand builds. Freight costs are also stranding heavy crude in the Americas, and Houthi strikes on Riyadh raise the risk of a wider war.

## Hormuz: 11 October count revised to six ships hit

Updated reports put the number of commercial vessels struck in and around the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October at six: five tankers and a container ship [gCaptain]. Earlier reports had the Petro Dolphin strike off Sohar and two more tankers inside the strait. This is a revised count for the same day, not evidence of a faster pace. The items carry no attacks on later dates.

Brent settled above $106 on Monday, with traders citing Middle East escalation and doubt over the Russia-Ukraine energy truce [Rigzone].

Separately, the UK's Maritime and Coastguard Agency issued guidance note MGN 719. It warns shipowners that satellite navigation is no longer reliable in some of the busiest shipping regions and tells crews to practise navigating without GPS [MCA via Splash247].

## Gulf LNG still down over 75%, Asia outbids Europe

LNG flows from the Persian Gulf remain more than 75% below their level before 28 February [OilPrice.com]. Heating demand is rising, and Europe's November gas contract is trading above €81/MWh [OilPrice.com]. Asian spot LNG has moved above European prices, drawing cargoes east and away from Europe. With coal about 80% cheaper than LNG [OilPrice.com], coal demand is rising.

GIE now shows EU storage at 73.8% full on 11 October, up 0.14 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. That revises the earlier 73.7% reading for the same date.

Possible knock-on: if Asia keeps outbidding Europe, European refiners and ammonia producers face higher gas costs into winter.

Sources: [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Natural-Gas/Global-Coal-Demand-Rises-as-LNG-Shortage-Deepens.html) · [GIE AGSI](https://agsi.gie.eu/)

## Freight spike strands heavy crude in the Americas

Soaring tanker rates have choked off exports of dense heavy crude, which is now piling up in the Americas [gCaptain]. US refiners are running close to their limits and cannot absorb the surplus [gCaptain]. The cost of shipping is now the binding constraint on these barrels, not demand for them.

The latest EIA data, for the week to 2 October, show:
- commercial crude stocks of 424,134 kb, down 3,186 kb [EIA]
- refinery utilisation of 92.7% [EIA]

The item gives no differentials or stock figures for the build-up.

Sources: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/soaring-tanker-rates-leave-americas-awash-in-crude-oil/) · [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/)

## Houthis strike Riyadh airport

Weekend attacks on Riyadh's main airport were deadly and show the Houthis can reach the centre of Saudi power [gCaptain]. Analysts quoted see a risk of escalation that draws in the US. The item reports no damage to energy infrastructure or exports.

Possible knock-on: Saudi Arabia's Red Sea export outlet at Yanbu, the main bypass for Hormuz, sits within the same threat range.

Sources: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/deadly-attacks-on-saudi-arabia-risk-wider-war-that-draws-in-us/)
