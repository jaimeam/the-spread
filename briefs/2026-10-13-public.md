# The Spread - Tuesday, 13 October 2026

## Hormuz: six ships hit in one day
**What changed:** gCaptain now reports six commercial vessels struck in and around the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October: five tankers and a container ship [gCaptain]. Our last edition carried two tankers hit inside the strait. The pace of attacks is rising, and the targets now include container shipping as well as tankers. Brent settled above $106 on Monday [Rigzone], in line with the $106.80 we reported yesterday.

**Chain:** more strikes on Gulf shipping → higher risk for owners lifting from the Gulf → freight that was already at record levels stays tight, and the supply-risk premium holds in Brent [Rigzone].

The items do not show how much Gulf crude and product export volume is actually lost. Tanker-tracking and loading data from Saudi and Iraqi terminals will show the scale.

## Houthis strike Riyadh's main airport
**New front:** Houthi attacks at the weekend hit Riyadh's main airport and caused deaths. gCaptain's analysis says this raises the risk of an escalation that draws in the US [gCaptain]. That assessment is gCaptain's view, not a confirmed policy move.

**Chain:** the Houthis have now shown they can reach deep into Saudi Arabia → the risk premium covers Saudi assets as well as Gulf shipping. *Possible knock-on:* Yanbu exports on the Red Sea, the bypass route around Hormuz, come into scope if Houthi targeting widens. Today's items do not report any attack on Yanbu.

## LNG: Asia outbids Europe, coal gains
Gulf LNG flows are still more than 75% below their level before 28 February [OilPrice.com]. The November European gas contract is trading above €81/MWh [OilPrice.com]. Asian spot LNG has risen above European prices, which is pulling cargoes east [OilPrice.com]. Coal is about 80% cheaper than LNG, and global coal demand is rising as a result [OilPrice.com].

**Chain:** Hormuz cuts Qatari and UAE LNG supply → Asian buyers outbid Europe for spot cargoes → Europe competes for fewer cargoes heading into winter, and buyers in both regions switch to coal where they can.

EU storage stood at 73.8% full on 11 October, up 0.14 points day on day [GIE AGSI]. This revises the 73.7% we carried yesterday.

Source: https://oilprice.com/Energy/Natural-Gas/Global-Coal-Demand-Rises-as-LNG-Shortage-Deepens.html

## Freight strands heavy crude in the Americas
High tanker rates have choked off exports of heavy crude from the Americas. At the same time, US refiners are running close to their limits, so the barrels are piling up in the region [gCaptain]. US refinery utilisation was 92.7% in the week to 2 October [EIA].

**Chain:** record freight → the cost of shipping Americas heavy crude to distant buyers becomes prohibitive → heavy crude backs up at home, while US refineries have little spare capacity to absorb it. This is the reverse of the Hormuz problem: medium and heavy sour crude is scarce east of Suez but stranded in the Americas.

Source: https://gcaptain.com/soaring-tanker-rates-leave-americas-awash-in-crude-oil/
