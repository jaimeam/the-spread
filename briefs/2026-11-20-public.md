# The Spread - Friday, 20 November 2026

The IEA puts the Middle East war's supply loss at 2.8bn barrels of oil and says there is no route back to the pre-Hormuz energy world. The EU has given gas and oil importers two more years before methane rules bite, and another tanker was struck in the strait overnight.

## War cost 2.8bn barrels

The IEA's World Energy Outlook, released Thursday, estimates the war has removed 2.8bn barrels of oil and 60 bcm of natural gas from global supply [IEA via OilPrice.com]. The agency finds no credible pathway back to the pre-crisis system. Under prolonged shipping risk, the need for flows through Hormuz could fall from 20 MMbpd to under 2 MMbpd by 2035, as buyers build around the strait. A clear resolution instead points to a buyer's market for oil and gas [IEA via World Oil]. Under the IEA's current-policies scenario, oil and gas demand keep growing [IEA via OilPrice.com].

This is an outlook, not a flow signal. Near-term data point the other way. The EIA says oil shipments through Middle East chokepoints rose 19% in the third quarter [EIA].

## EU delays methane rules

The European Parliament and member states agreed Thursday to push back the methane regulation by two years [OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. Without the delay, rules requiring importers to document methane emissions on imported oil and gas would have started at the beginning of 2027 [Rigzone]. Importers had warned they could not source enough compliant supply in a strained market. OilPrice.com also reports pressure from the US, Europe's top oil and gas supplier.

The effect is to keep US LNG and crude flowing into Europe without a new paperwork and verification cost. That matters while storage keeps drawing: EU storage was 69.8% full on 18 November, down 0.12 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

Separately, a Commission draft seen by Bloomberg would require member states to align emergency oil reserves with national fuel consumption and refining capacity [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. The draft follows a war that showed crude in storage does not guarantee enough diesel or jet. It is a draft only and has not been adopted. Possible knock-on: product-specific stockholding could add to European demand for middle distillates.

## Another tanker struck in Hormuz

UKMTO issued a fresh warning overnight Wednesday after reports that an unidentified tanker was hit in the strait [UKMTO via Splash247]. That follows the ADNOC L&S vessel attacked on 17 November. Splash247 counts two seafarers missing and more than a dozen injured this week. gCaptain reports Iran's attacks on tankers are intensifying. The tanker's name, owner and damage aren't in today's reports; UKMTO or owner updates will settle that.

Prices barely moved. Brent traded at $92.01/bbl and WTI at $83.00 on Thursday morning [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone describes prices swinging as Iran tensions offset improving Middle East supply. The market is weighing attacks against forecasts of a glut. OilPrice.com also notes that tanker-tracking data still show Gulf outflows below pre-war levels, and an analysis there argues a shortage of inbound empty tankers could cap exports.

Traders are looking past the risk. Major trading houses are in talks with QatarEnergy for 2027 term supply, mostly Qatar Marine and Land [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. This rests on unnamed sources and QatarEnergy has not commented.

## India imports record crude

India imported a record 5.58m b/d of crude in October, according to trade data [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. That is up 4.7% on September and 6.9% on October 2025. Saudi volumes reached an eight-month high as Gulf shipments recovered. Gulf barrels returning through Hormuz are going straight into Indian refineries, but they are adding to Russian supply rather than displacing it. Russia remained India's largest supplier at 1.85m b/d, up 3.5% month on month.
