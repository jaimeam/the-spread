# The Spread - Friday, 30 October 2026

Europe plans to release 42m bbl of emergency oil stocks over three months, half of it diesel, the most direct relief yet for its record diesel prices. A tanker was left burning and largely submerged off Oman, while a US approach to Tehran pulled Brent back towards $114.

## Europe to release 42m bbl

France's economy minister Roland Lescure says Europe will release 42m bbl of emergency stocks over the next three months, half of it diesel [OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. That averages roughly 460,000 b/d [OilPrice.com]. France's share includes 10m bbl of diesel, which Lescure says will start reaching the market within days. It will be sold at cost, and he expects pump prices to fall by 12-18 cents a litre from as early as next week [OilPrice.com]. The figures come from Paris alone. No other government or the IEA has confirmed them, and the items don't say whether the barrels come from the IEA's final 100m bbl tranche.

The diesel is aimed at Europe's tightest barrel. Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen said on Wednesday that two decades of EU refinery closures had been a mistake, with diesel at all-time highs. EU gas touched €100/MWh the same day, and energy ministers met on Thursday on winter prices [OilPrice.com]. Over three months, the stock diesel competes directly with imported cargoes. US Gulf barrels have already been priced out of Europe by freight. Possible knock-on: softer NWE diesel cracks as the French barrels land.

Country shares beyond France's aren't in today's reports. National stockholding agencies' release notices will show them.

## Tanker sinking off Oman after projectile strike

A tanker was left burning and largely submerged after a suspected projectile strike about 9 nautical miles north of Khasab, on Oman's Musandam Peninsula [Vanguard via Splash247]. UK maritime authorities confirmed reports of a vessel on fire off the Omani coast. The ship's name, its cargo and the attacker are not reported, and the sinking itself is not confirmed. This is the narrowest part of the strait, where the US has already told ships to avoid unnecessary transits.

Security worries are reaching the industry's calendar. Exxon CEO Darren Woods and Chevron upstream president Clay Neff have cancelled plans to attend ADIPEC in Abu Dhabi on 2-5 November, according to sources [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. Washington is weighing further military action against Iran.

## Brent slips on Vance outreach

Brent traded at $114.14 and WTI at $101.45 on Thursday, down from Wednesday [OilPrice.com]. The Wall Street Journal reported that Vice President Vance had written to Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary to restart talks. Tehran's response is not reported. Later in the day, crude whipsawed on conflicting reports about US military plans [Rigzone].

## Record refining margins show up in Q3 profits

Third-quarter results put numbers on the margin windfall:

- **Shell:** adjusted earnings of $10.814bn, the highest since 2022, against $5.432bn a year ago, plus a $5bn buyback [OilPrice.com; Rigzone].
- **TotalEnergies:** $9.1bn, up 127% y/y, and it expects LNG prices and refining margins to hold up [OilPrice.com; Rigzone].
- **Phillips 66:** net profit of $5.62bn, up from $3.85bn in Q2 [Rigzone].
- **Sinopec:** profit up 63.6% to $2bn, though it couldn't fully capture global margins [OilPrice.com].

Consumers carry the other side. The EU has paid about $11bn a month more for oil since the strait closed [Euronews via OilPrice.com]. The value of EU petroleum imports rose 55.8% in Q2 on broadly stable volumes [Eurostat via OilPrice.com].

## Qatar adds a 20-year US LNG supply

QatarEnergy signed a 20-year deal with Woodside for LNG from its Louisiana project [Offshore Energy]. CEO Saad al-Kaabi told Reuters the company is buying long-term US and other supply so it can earn revenue if Hormuz exports are disrupted again [gCaptain]. Cheniere raised its full-year outlook on demand for US cargoes [gCaptain]. Kaabi's remarks don't address this week's buyer-reported extension of Qatari force majeure to year-end.

## Russian diesel tanker now tracked to New York for 7 November

Ship-tracking data now has Agioi Fanendes, carrying nearly 40,000 t of ULSD from Primorsk, arriving at New York/New Jersey on 7 November [OilPrice.com]. Earlier reports had it showing "for orders" with Norfolk named for 2 November. The buyer is still unidentified. Senator Blumenthal has asked the owner to name everyone involved by 31 October.

## Boxship sunk off Bulgaria

The Greek-managed Contship Jet sank after three sea drones hit it about 17 nautical miles east of Cape Kaliakra, on a voyage from Haifa to Constanta [Bulgarian government via gCaptain; Splash247]. Russia claims further attacks on ships bound for Ukrainian ports. Those claims are unconfirmed.
