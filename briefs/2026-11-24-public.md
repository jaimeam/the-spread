# The Spread - Tuesday, 24 November 2026

Libya lost about 350,000 b/d of output after armed groups shut the Sharara pipeline. Crude still fell nearly 4% on Monday as returning Gulf supply and talk of Iran diplomacy outweighed it. European gas jumped on a cold forecast, with storage now below 69%.

## Libya loses 350,000 b/d

An armed group closed a valve on the pipeline from Sharara, Libya's largest field, cutting national production by about 350,000 b/d [NOC via OilPrice.com]. The National Oil Corporation (NOC) condemned the closure. It comes on top of a separate shutdown of the Hamada pipeline, now in its third week, which has already cost about 15,000 b/d [NOC via OilPrice.com].

The outage removes prompt barrels just as the market is pricing a surplus. Monday's price action shows the loss is not yet setting direction. No restart timeline is reported; further NOC statements on the Sharara valve will show how long the barrels stay out.

## Cold forecast lifts TTF 8.6%

Dutch TTF opened the week at €96.075/MWh, up 8.6% from Friday, on forecasts of colder weather [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. That is below the late-October four-year high above €100/MWh but nearly triple the €30/MWh seen before the war [OilPrice.com].

The cold arrives with EU storage at 68.9% full on 22 November, down 0.24 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. Stronger heating demand on thin stocks raises Europe's pull on LNG. That competition is already showing in Asia:

- **Japan:** day-ahead power rose to its highest since August 2022 on expected gas demand in Chubu and Hokkaido [JEPX via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].
- **South Korea:** the trade ministry asked Qatar to prioritise Korean LNG once Hormuz traffic normalises. Qatar has not responded publicly [South Korean ministry via OilPrice.com].

Qatari flows have recovered far less than Gulf crude [OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: higher gas costs feed through to refinery fuel and hydrogen costs in Europe.

On supply, Norway produced about 12.56 bcf/d in October, a nine-month high and up about a fifth on September (preliminary official figures) [Norwegian data via Rigzone].

## WTI sinks to lowest since August

At 10:29 ET Monday, WTI was down 3.5% at $80.50/bbl, its weakest since late August. Brent was down 3.6% at $89.23 [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone put WTI's fall at 4.5% by the close [Rigzone].

Two forces drove the move:

- **Supply:** returning Gulf supply outweighed continued tanker attacks [OilPrice.com].
- **Diplomacy:** Trump signalled more time for diplomacy with Iran [Rigzone].

Trump's message cut both ways. He told Fox News that major strikes on Iran could resume after the New Year if no deal is reached, and he dismissed the indirect talks as going nowhere. Tehran has not confirmed any negotiating position, so this is Trump's account only [OilPrice.com; Rigzone].

Forecasters are split on 2027. Bank of America kept its 2027 Brent forecast at $80, citing low inventories and disruption risk [BofA via OilPrice.com]. Macquarie strategists said the oil narrative appears to have turned [Rigzone].

## Treasury hits Iran oil payment networks

The US Treasury sanctioned financial networks that support Iran's oil exports and tanker operations. Targets include companies accused of handling payments for the National Iranian Tanker Company [US Treasury via gCaptain; OFAC].

Separately, an Iranian tanker with no track record signalled on Monday that it had passed the US blockade outside Hormuz, a route analysts consider unlikely. The episode shows how little anyone can verify about traffic in the area [gCaptain].

The Joint Maritime Information Center has now identified two of the three tankers hit over the weekend. The ships' identities are not given in today's items [JMIC via Splash247].

## Russian Baltic loadings slip

Storms have delayed more than 10 tankers at Russia's key Baltic ports this month [traders via Reuters via OilPrice.com]. Ukrainian drone attacks and local attack alerts have added to delays at both Baltic and Black Sea terminals.

Ukraine's maritime campaign is also widening:

- **Benghazi:** Zelenskiy says naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in the Libyan port. That would be Kyiv's first admitted strike on Libyan soil, on the Mediterranean route that carries Russian oil to Asia. Moscow has not commented in today's items [Zelenskiy via OilPrice.com; gCaptain].
- **Baltic:** the drone-damaged cargo ship Sparta II has been towed out of Finnish waters [gCaptain].

## ConocoPhillips exits Norway for $4.4bn

Vår Energi will buy ConocoPhillips' Norwegian business for $4.425bn, including its Teesside terminal stake [World Oil]. The assets include:

- operatorship of the Greater Ekofisk Area
- the Norpipe export system
- the Teesside terminal in the UK

The business produced about 120,000 boe/d in 2025 [World Oil; Splash247].

## US refiners squeezed their own gasoline margin

US refiners cut gasoline yields below 45% on average in the first eight months of 2026, under the prior five-year low in every month, to chase diesel and jet [EIA via OilPrice.com]. Jet's share of output topped 12% in March for the first time and held there through August [EIA via OilPrice.com]. Higher crude runs still produced enough gasoline to nearly erase gasoline margins [OilPrice.com].
