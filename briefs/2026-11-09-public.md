# The Spread - Monday, 9 November 2026

Iraq says Washington has agreed a lane for its oil exports through Hormuz near Oman, while US forces disabled a ship trying to run the Iran blockade. Crude ended last week with its steepest weekly loss since 2022 as peace bets outran the fighting.

## Iraq claims Hormuz lane deal

Iraq's foreign minister, Fuad Hussein, said on Sunday that Baghdad has reached understandings with the United States allowing Iraqi crude to transit Hormuz through a shipping lane near Oman [Reuters via gCaptain]. Only Baghdad has said this. No US statement is in the reports. If it holds, Iraqi medium-sour barrels would move under a US-sanctioned route rather than ad hoc transits.

Neither the lane's exact route nor any volume is reported. Basrah loading data in the coming days will show whether the lane is being used.

The claim lands as Gulf supply recovers. A Bloomberg survey puts October OPEC output close to pre-war levels, led by Saudi Arabia [Bloomberg via Rigzone]. Iraq's oil minister said the country already produces more than 5m b/d, over half a million above its OPEC+ requirement [OilPrice.com]. That is awkward six days after Baghdad agreed to hold December targets unchanged. Ships are now the binding constraint for some buyers. HPCL's chairman says the Indian refiner is struggling to hire tankers for Gulf loadings even with crude flows near pre-war levels [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

## Sixth Russian refinery hit

Ukraine has struck a sixth Russian refinery within a week [Rigzone], up from three hit between 30 October and 2 November. These are new strikes, not a recount. Damage and idled capacity are not reported.

The pressure shows in Moscow's budget. Subsidies paid to producers to keep road fuels at home are at their highest in more than four years [Rigzone]. With the diesel export ban already extended to end-January, more lost runs mean less Russian product reaching export markets.

Separately, Russia says overnight strikes hit three cargo ships carrying military goods for Ukraine, plus two substations [Reuters via gCaptain]. That is Moscow's claim only, with no ship names or Kyiv response.

## US disables blockade runner off Oman

On Saturday a US military aircraft fired on and disabled a commercial vessel trying to break the blockade of Iran in the Gulf of Oman [CENTCOM via Reuters via gCaptain]. The same evening, explosions hit southern Iran. Iranian media framed them as Iran's own military activity rather than US strikes, and both sides reported ships hit [Reuters via gCaptain].

Earlier, a seafarer was killed on Thursday in a suspected missile strike on the Velos Sapphire, a Greek-managed product tanker of 74,998 dwt, inside the Gulf [ship manager via gCaptain; Splash247]. Iran has also reshuffled its Hormuz blacklist [Splash247].

FGE NexantECA says Iranian exports are near zero under the blockade. It sees slim odds of a deal and calls the next two months the period of highest escalation risk [FGE via gCaptain].

## Peace bets drive steepest weekly loss since 2022

Brent finished the week around $99, down about 15% [OilPrice.com], the biggest weekly drop since 2022 [Rigzone]. December WTI lost $11.37 from its $102.39 open and touched $86.65 [OilPrice.com].

The trigger was political. Republicans lost both chambers of Congress, and Vice President Vance blamed the war [OilPrice.com], which fed bets on de-escalation. Bank balances point the same way. JPMorgan estimates a 1.1m b/d surplus in September widening to 1.4m b/d in October as high prices cut demand [JPMorgan via OilPrice.com].

Product markets moved first. Asia's high-sulphur fuel oil margin fell to a $2.29/bbl discount on Thursday [OilPrice.com], as more Gulf oil reaches Asia and traders price an easier exit from the strait.

## Traders seek sanctioned tankers as freight bites

Trafigura, Vitol, Mercuria and ADNOC are negotiating with US officials for licences to buy or lease about 50 sanctioned tankers, and Treasury is weighing approvals [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. The report rests on unnamed sources, and Treasury has not commented.

The freight squeeze is reshaping crude pricing. Soaring tanker rates have forced sellers to deepen discounts on heavy grades, which hands US Gulf Coast refiners cheaper feedstock [Reuters via gCaptain]. US imports of Venezuelan crude averaged 701,000 b/d in July, the most since May 2017, before easing to 655,000 b/d in August [EIA]. Possible knock-on: if licences are granted, extra tonnage could narrow those heavy-crude discounts.

## Chinese diesel exports seen lower

Energy Aspects now expects China to export about 580,000 t of diesel in November, 400,000 t below its previous estimate, because domestic inventories have fallen [Energy Aspects via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. The consultancy says the figure could be revised up if domestic demand weakens. Fewer Chinese barrels leave Asia's diesel market tighter.

## Gas draws accelerate; Egypt cuts fertiliser supply

EU storage stood at 72.7% full on 7 November, down 0.33 points on the day [GIE AGSI], against 73.8% on 4 November. Egypt has told fertiliser producers their gas supply will be cut by a further 33% [company statement via Reuters via gCaptain]. Norway's regulator Havtil is investigating the Visund switchboard-room fire [World Oil].
