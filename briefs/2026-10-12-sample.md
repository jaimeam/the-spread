## The one thing

**Diesel is still the market's pressure point, and today's headline relief targets the wrong bottleneck.** Brent swung between roughly $103 and $106 [Business Standard, TradingKey]. It fell early after Washington said Russia and Ukraine agreed to stop hitting each other's energy sites, alongside a US diesel-supply deal with Moscow. It recovered as Hormuz risk reasserted itself. Even if the truce holds, damaged Russian units take weeks to restart, and the Gulf diesel exporters remain constrained.

## 1. Russia–Ukraine "energy truce", take two

**What happened.** US officials say Kyiv and Moscow have agreed to halt strikes on energy infrastructure, tied to a US arrangement for Russian diesel supply. Brent dropped about $1.5 in Asian hours on the news [Business Standard].

**Fact vs claim.** A near-identical announcement on 14 September was not confirmed by either side. Zelensky then said Ukraine would stop only if Russia gave genuine guarantees, and he doubted Moscow would comply [NewsTribune/AP]. Treat today's version as unconfirmed until both capitals say so.

**Chain.** Fewer drone strikes → Russian CDUs restart over weeks → Moscow could relax its product export ban (as of early September, non-producers were barred from exporting diesel until January 2027 [S&P Global]) → more Russian diesel to Turkey, Brazil and Africa → less competition for US Gulf barrels → European diesel cracks ease. Each step has a lag, so the first effect is sentiment, not supply.

Sources: [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/markets/commodities/oil-falls-on-hopes-russia-ukraine-energy-ceasefire-will-boost-supplies-126101200047_1.html) · [AP via NewsTribune](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/sep/15/trump-claims-russia-ukraine-energy-truce-kyiv/) · [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/090326-european-diesel-margins-surge-as-us-supply-crunch-supports-rally)

## 2. Hormuz: still no deal

**What happened.** Iran is still reviewing Washington's reply on reopening the Strait, and attacks on tankers and aviation sites kept a floor under crude [TradingKey]. There are also reports of an attack on Riyadh's airport, with Washington weighing a role in Saudi retaliation against the Houthis [Trading Economics] *(single source; confirm)*.

**Chain.** Hormuz constraint → Gulf medium-sour crude and Gulf diesel/jet exports curtailed → Europe leans harder on US Gulf diesel → NWE and NY Harbor cracks compete for the same barrels. A Red Sea escalation would threaten Saudi Arabia's Yanbu bypass route, which is the system's safety valve.

**Context.** In mid-September a pipeline disruption briefly sent the Brent spot price to $131 on 15 September [EIA STEO]. Around then, Saudi Arabia's East-West line was shut by drone attacks and exports had to be rerouted back through the Gulf [Bloomberg via WorldOil].

Sources: [TradingKey](https://tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262211557-us-premarket-hormuz-oil-salesforce-memory-chips-micron-tradingkey) · [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil) · [WorldOil/Bloomberg](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2026/9/21/brent-falls-to-100-as-strait-of-hormuz-oil-flows-increase/)

## 3. The numbers that frame Q4

- **EIA (6 Oct STEO):** Brent forecast at $105 for 4Q26, up $14 from last month, explicitly because extreme diesel tightness is pulling refiners into buying more crude. It sees $84 in 2027. US retail diesel averaged $6.29/gal in September. The forecast includes the 40 million barrel SPR exchange but **not** the G7's 2 October measures [EIA / Hydrocarbon Engineering].
- **Goldman Sachs:** December Brent target raised to $90 from $85, with a risk premium put at just over $20/bbl [Business Standard].
- **Diesel cracks:** the US ULSD futures crack peaked at $118/bbl on 16 September [WION, citing Trading Economics]. The ARA diesel crack set a record of $98/b in early September [S&P Global].

**Read-across.** My read: Goldman's ~$20 risk premium is roughly the market's gap between "Hormuz deal" and "no deal". Refining margins carry more of the stress than crude does. For refiners with distillate-heavy yields, Q4 earnings will look strong whatever Brent does.

Sources: [Hydrocarbon Engineering (EIA release)](https://www.hydrocarbonengineering.com/refining/08102026/eia-october-steo/) · [EIA STEO PDF](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/oct26.pdf) · [WION](https://www.wionews.com/world/global-diesel-crisis-supply-crack-spreads-record-high-1791049895459)

## 4. OPEC+ on hold, and seven, not eight

The voluntary-cut group met on 4 October and kept November required production at the September level. Next meeting: 1 November [OPEC]. Worth noting for newer readers: the group is now **seven** countries; the UAE no longer appears in it. Paper quotas matter less than usual while physical exports from the Gulf are constrained, so watch loadings, not communiqués.

Source: [OPEC press release, 4 Oct](https://opec.org/pr-detail/616-4-october-2026.html)
