# The Spread - Monday, 2 November 2026

An LNG carrier cleared by Iran was attacked near Hormuz, and limpet mines have hit a tanker for the first time in the war. European gas has topped €101/MWh, Brent ended October near $118, and Russia has kept its diesel export ban in place through January.

## Pakistan LNG ship attacked

Pakistan had negotiated safe passage with Iran for the LNG carrier Al Mayeda before it was attacked near the Strait of Hormuz [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. The report doesn't say who attacked the ship or how badly it was damaged. If a cargo cleared by Tehran can still be hit, buyers have no reliable way to buy safe transit, which removes one workaround for moving Gulf LNG.

The method of attack has also changed. Late on Thursday 29 October, a small boat attached two explosive devices to the hull of the Panama-flagged Louise 1, a 159,092 dwt tanker, 25nm northeast of Khasab [Splash247]. The blasts started an engine-room fire and temporarily disabled the ship. Maritime security sources say this is the first successful limpet-mine attack in the strait since the war began in February [gCaptain]. UKMTO logged it as the third report that day of an outbound tanker on fire [UKMTO via OilPrice.com].

On Friday the IRGC Navy said four tankers caught fire and exploded while using what it called unauthorised routes [IRGC via Reuters via gCaptain]. That is Iran's claim alone. No owner, flag state or navy has confirmed it.

## EU gas tops €101/MWh

Front-month TTF went above €101/MWh on Friday, the first time since 2022. It rose 4% by mid-morning and was on track for an 8% weekly gain [OilPrice.com]. Traders are pricing the risk that Qatari and Emirati LNG stays choked off at Hormuz just as winter starts with storage low.

Storage is now falling. It was 75.0% full on 27 October and 74.6% on 31 October, down 0.06 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. Higher gas prices raise refinery fuel and hydrogen costs and, through gas-to-oil switching, support diesel and fuel oil.

In Asia, Japan is set to overtake China as the largest LNG importer this year, at 62m t against 61m t [ICIS via OilPrice.com]. High prices and adequate Chinese storage are holding back China's buying. On the supply side, Shell took the first long-term contract cargo from Venture Global's Plaquemines after Phase 1 commissioning [Reuters via gCaptain].

## Brent ends October near $118

Brent hit $117 on Thursday after the Louise 1 attack [OilPrice.com]. It was trading around $118 on Friday, heading for a 13-14% monthly rise and a fourth straight monthly gain [OilPrice.com].

Several things are supporting the price:
- **US strikes:** Trump has repeatedly suggested strikes on Iran could resume after Tuesday's midterms [OilPrice.com].
- **Iran's Jask port:** explosions there have been reported but not confirmed [OilPrice.com].
- **Forecasts:** ANZ raised its end-2026 Brent forecast by $10 to $105, assuming current export and shipping constraints last through December [ANZ via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

The Houthis claim new strikes on the Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries and an Aramco facility at Abqaiq. Riyadh has not confirmed the attacks or any damage [OilPrice.com].

## Russian diesel ban runs to January

Russia has extended its general ban on diesel exports by producers to the end of January, though some volumes may still flow [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. That keeps Turkey, Brazil and African buyers competing with Europe for US and Indian diesel.

US barrels are not reaching Europe either. Spot USGC-UKC freight hit 465 WS, its highest since April, keeping transatlantic diesel arbs shut for a second week, as of a 25 October note [Kpler]. The West Coast India-to-Med arb was about $4/t from opening at the time [Kpler]. Possible knock-on: Indian barrels may be the first to move if Med freight eases.

Sources: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/russia-extends-ban-on-most-diesel-exports-through-january/) · [Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/economics-improve-but-arbs-remain-closed-into-europe)

## OPEC+ leaves December quotas unchanged

At Sunday's meeting, seven OPEC+ members kept December targets unchanged, as expected [World Oil]. The decision changes little in practice: Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq are still producing well below quota because of Hormuz constraints [World Oil].
