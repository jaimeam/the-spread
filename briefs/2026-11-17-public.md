# The Spread - Tuesday, 17 November 2026

UKMTO has independently logged two tanker attacks in Hormuz, while Houthi gains near Bab el-Mandeb put the Saudi Red Sea bypass route in question. Crude still fell on glut forecasts, and Europe's gas price eased as US LNG feedgas hit a record.

## UKMTO logs two Hormuz attacks

Two tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, according to separate UKMTO warnings. Both had engine-room fires and one was left disabled [UKMTO via gCaptain]. Recent hits had rested on Iranian claims alone; this is a maritime-security confirmation of attacks on a single day. The IRGC separately says two tankers struck mines on Monday after using a southern route Iran has not authorised. It says US officers guided them, with fighter-jet support [IRGC via Mehr via OilPrice.com]. That account is Iran's alone. The reports don't identify the attacker or say whether UKMTO's two ships are the IRGC's two; owner or flag-state statements would settle it.

Iran also struck Bahrain for the second time in three days, while saying a path to ending the conflict stays open [Rigzone]. The price response was muted: one market account says the war has not stopped crude reaching the market [gCaptain]. VLCC rates have fallen more than 60% from October's record as the risk premium unwinds [gCaptain].

## Houthis close in on Aden

Houthi fighters are within 100 km of Aden after government forces pulled back from positions near the Bab el-Mandeb, according to Yemeni military sources [Yemeni military sources via OilPrice.com]. The Houthis have not given their own account. The strait matters because Saudi crude loaded at Yanbu passes through it to Asia. Yanbu is the outlet that bypasses Hormuz, so any threat to the strait reaches the Saudi barrels that have been replacing Gulf loadings. Murban rose 1.52% to $94.89 on Monday morning while Brent fell 2.32% to $93.62 [OilPrice.com].

Container lines are moving the other way. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd are returning three more Gemini services to Suez [gCaptain].

## Glut calls pull crude down 3%

Oil fell more than 3% on Monday as supply growth outweighed Middle East risk [Rigzone]. The EIA now expects a global oil glut this quarter [EIA via Rigzone]. Goldman sees supply exceeding demand by 2m b/d in 2027. It expects Brent to average $88 next year and slide toward $78 by year-end, citing weaker Chinese transport-fuel demand [Goldman Sachs via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. New supply is arriving: ExxonMobil started Uaru, its fifth Stabroek project, lifting Guyana's installed capacity to 1.1m b/d [World Oil].

The Gulf crude pricing picture is softening. Kpler reports Mideast Gulf crude discounts deepening as Asian refiners turn to Atlantic barrels [Kpler]. Chinese independent refiners are seeking Iraqi cargoes through trading houses, because the US blockade has halted Iranian supply and Russian crude has become dearer [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Iraq is also trucking crude out via Syria [OilPrice.com].

Product markets are lagging the crude move. US diesel has dropped below $6/gal, while jet fuel stays high just as airline hedges expire [OilPrice.com].

## Record US LNG feedgas cools TTF

Pipeline flows to US LNG plants hit a record 21.8 bcf on Saturday, up 12% year on year. Golden Pass reached a record and Sabine Pass its highest since April [BloombergNEF via OilPrice.com]. With milder weather forecast, front-month TTF fell 3.3% to €87.965/MWh on Monday morning [OilPrice.com].

Storage remains the constraint. EU stocks were 70.4% full on 15 November, down 0.16 points [GIE AGSI]. Net withdrawals so far equal about 10% of the 51.2 bcm injected this summer [APA-Economics via OilPrice.com].

UK supply news is mixed. Gas from Jackdaw is now flowing to St Fergus via Shearwater [Adura via OilPrice.com]. Against that, the Apache pay dispute has escalated to a standstill on its North Sea platforms; volumes are not reported [Offshore Energy]. In Norway, the Supreme Court invalidated approvals for Breidablikk, Tyrving and Yggdrasil over emissions assessments [Splash247].

## EU widens shadow-fleet sanctions

The EU's new Russia package targets shipowners, managers, insurers and the financial networks behind the shadow fleet [gCaptain]. Hitting insurance and management, not just hulls, narrows the pool of services Russian export cargoes can use. Separately, a Somali-flagged wheat carrier capsized off Bulgaria after a reported sea-drone attack [gCaptain].
