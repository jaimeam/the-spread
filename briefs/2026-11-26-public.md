# The Spread - Thursday, 26 November 2026

Houthi drones over Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery reversed Wednesday's early slide and lifted Brent above $93. Separately, a record share of large product tankers has switched to hauling crude, which keeps the cost of moving diesel and jet high.

## Drones downed over Ras Tanura

The Houthis claimed a strike on Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest refinery. Saudi Arabia says its air defences destroyed four drones over the site on Wednesday morning. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said fragments fell from the interceptions, with no injuries in an initial assessment [OilPrice.com]. The Houthi claim of a hit is theirs alone. Riyadh's account is of interceptions only.

Crude turned on the news. In early Asian trade Brent was at $90.10 and WTI at $80.71 [OilPrice.com]. By 10:16 ET Brent was at $93.60, up 2.41%, and WTI at $83.51, up 1.78% [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone described the gain as coming in thin pre-holiday trading.

The items do not say whether any refinery units or loadings at Ras Tanura were affected. An Aramco or Saudi operational statement would settle that.

Possible knock-on: Houthi reach to Saudi Arabia's Gulf coast would put both Saudi export coasts at risk, since the Houthis have already ordered ships out of Saudi Red Sea ports.

## Record 75% of LR2s haul crude

A record 75% of the global LR2 fleet is now carrying crude rather than clean products. The number and share of clean vessels are at record lows [OilPrice.com].

The switch follows earnings. Crude pays more after the Middle East disruption, so owners move LR2s into dirty trade. That shrinks the pool of large clean tankers and keeps fuel freight costs elevated [gCaptain]. Long-haul diesel and jet buyers pay for it, Europe in particular. The transatlantic MR arbitrage was already shut for December arrivals as of Kpler's mid-November note.

## IRGC claims two more mine hits; ADNOC restricts tankers

The IRGC Navy says two tankers hit mines early Wednesday off the southern and eastern coasts of the strait, causing engine-room fires. It says both ships were on routes Iran has not authorised [IRGC via OilPrice.com]. This is Iran's claim only; no ships are named.

ADNOC has placed restrictions on loaded crude and product tankers entering waters near some major UAE export hubs [Rigzone]. Which hubs are affected, and for how long, is not given.

In a note dated 19 November, Kpler put Hormuz flows at 55% of pre-war levels. It said Asian refiners are paying large premiums for Atlantic Basin crude and chasing 2027 term deals with QatarEnergy and ADNOC [Kpler].

## China restocks at lower prices

Chinese refiners are buying more crude abroad to rebuild inventories after the price fall, according to traders cited by Bloomberg [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Sinopec alone has reportedly bought about 20m bbl of West African crude this month for December loading. All Chinese buyers together take 20-25m bbl of West African crude in an average month [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Purchases from the Middle East and Brazil are also up. The extra demand supports Atlantic Basin grades, the same barrels Asian buyers are already paying premiums for.

## US distillate stocks rise a third week

In the week to 20 November, US stocks rose across the board [EIA]:
- **Commercial crude:** up 1,109 kb to 418,906 kb, 3% below the five-year average [EIA via OilPrice.com].
- **Gasoline:** up 1,733 kb to 217,682 kb.
- **Distillates:** up 715 kb to 105,616 kb, a third straight weekly build from 103,330 kb in the week to 6 November [EIA].

Pump prices are easing. The US gasoline average was about $3.66/gal on 21 November, with diesel still well above it [OilPrice.com].

## UK price cap jumps 26%

Ofgem confirmed the household energy price cap will rise from £1,723 to £2,166 on 1 January, up 26%. That is the highest in over three years, driven by war-lifted wholesale gas prices [Ofgem via OilPrice.com].

Supply is not expected to ease soon. Petronas expects the LNG market to stay relatively tight through 2027 [Petronas via Bernama via OilPrice.com]. EU storage was 67.8% full on 24 November, down 0.56 points in a day [GIE AGSI].

On contracts, Equinor signed an 18-year gas supply deal with Germany's VNG, its longest Norwegian gas sale since the 1986 Troll agreements [Equinor via Rigzone]. On the Russian side, Dynagas told the SEC that $390m of charter revenue is at risk if it cannot replace insurance for two Yamal LNG vessels before UK sanctions tighten [Dynagas via Splash247].
