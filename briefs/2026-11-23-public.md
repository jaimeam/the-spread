# The Spread - Monday, 23 November 2026

US sour crude has lost its war premium and Gulf barrels are pouring into China, so oil posted a third weekly loss even as tankers kept getting hit near Oman. The Houthis have also told ships to leave Saudi Red Sea ports, putting Yanbu, the Hormuz bypass, in their sights.

## Sour crude premium vanishes

The price of US sour crude has plunged. Supply from Venezuela, Canada and the Middle East is surging and gives refiners plenty of barrels to choose from [Bloomberg via gCaptain; Rigzone]. Neither report gives the size of the move.

The pull is clearest in China. Imports from Gulf producers other than Iran reached 19.7m t in October, up 42.4% from September and the highest since the war began [SCMP calculation from Chinese customs via OilPrice.com]. Gulf producers cut prices to win buyers while freight sat at record highs [OilPrice.com].

For sour-heavy refiners on the US Gulf Coast and in Asia, the scarcity that hurt their margins for most of the year has turned into a choice of cheap barrels. Possible knock-on: the light-sweet premium could widen again as medium-sour supply comes back.

Prices followed. Brent fell about 5% on the week to around $91 [OilPrice.com], its third straight weekly loss [Rigzone]. January WTI lost more than 5% [OilPrice.com]. Brent was $92.18 and WTI $82.73 early Friday [OilPrice.com]. US oil rigs rose by 6 to 469, and the total count reached 607 [Baker Hughes via OilPrice.com].

## Three tankers hit since Friday

- **Friday:** a tanker was struck by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire [gCaptain].
- **Saturday:** an outbound tanker was hit on the stern by an unidentified projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman. A separate vessel reportedly struck a mine east of the strait [Reuters via gCaptain].

None of these reports names the attacker. Bloomberg's tally shows attacks peaked in October at the highest level of the war and have continued almost daily in November [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].

Hormuz crude volumes have dipped in recent days [OilPrice.com]. The reports don't say whether the attacks caused the dip; the next weekly tanker-tracking counts will show whether the decline holds.

Indian refiners are rushing to lock in tankers on long-term charters [Rigzone].

## Houthis warn ships off Saudi Red Sea ports

The Saudi-led coalition says it struck Hodeidah port on Friday to destroy Houthi capabilities. The Houthis responded by ordering ships to leave Saudi Red Sea ports, warning airlines away from Saudi airspace and promising new strikes [gCaptain].

Earlier Friday, media reported Houthi strikes on Riyadh, including the financial district, the sovereign wealth fund's headquarters and a petrochemicals company. These reports come from media only; no Saudi confirmation is cited. Brent briefly rose to $92.67 on the reports [OilPrice.com].

Yanbu is the Red Sea outlet Saudi Arabia uses to bypass Hormuz, so a credible threat there would hit the route that has carried diverted barrels. No effect on loadings is reported.

## Iranian cargo moves ship-to-ship

Last week Washington extended its naval blockade on Iranian ports to cover ship-to-ship transfers. This week a cargo of Iranian crude moved inside the Gulf from a sanctioned Suezmax to an unsanctioned medium-range tanker [UANI and Kpler via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. UANI is an anti-Iran advocacy group. OilPrice reads the transfer as Iran preparing to restart exports; Tehran has not confirmed this.

US Treasury officials met regulators in Singapore and Malaysia to warn them about Iran sanctions compliance [gCaptain]. Both countries are hubs for transfers of Iranian crude bound for China. OFAC also issued new Iran designations on 20 November; the notice gives no names [OFAC].

Separately, Iran again refused UN inspectors access to its nuclear sites [OilPrice.com].

## Saudi Arabia to refill Japan's reserve

Under a deal both governments announced Friday, Japan will buy up to 8m bbl of Saudi crude to rebuild the national reserve it drew down during the Hormuz crisis, if market conditions are favourable. Saudi Arabia gets temporary use of a further 16m bbl of Japanese storage, on top of the 8m bbl it already holds there, and has begun refilling those tanks [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; Rigzone].

## Norway lifts gas sales as EU storage slips

EU storage fell to 69.2% full on 21 November, from 69.8% on 18 November [GIE AGSI]. Net withdrawals already equal about 11% of the 51.2 bcm injected this summer [GIE via APA-Economics via OilPrice.com].

Norway is partly offsetting the draw. It sold 11 bcm in October, up 2.1 bcm from September, averaging 355.6 mcm/d [Norwegian Offshore Directorate via OilPrice.com].
