# The Spread - Wednesday, 11 November 2026

Ukrainian drone strikes have disrupted about a third of Russia's refining capacity just as Moscow tries to export more diesel. Brent briefly fell below $90 on Iran de-escalation signals, but freight and attacks on Gulf shipping are keeping delivered costs high.

## Russia loses 30% of refining

Two industry sources say Ukrainian drone attacks since late October have cut Russia's oil refining capacity by about 30% [Reuters via gCaptain]. Nine of Russia's 35 major refineries were disrupted over the past week. Together they have about 2.2m b/d of nameplate capacity [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. The hit lands as Moscow tries to raise diesel exports under an agreement with Washington, so fewer barrels are available just as more are being promised abroad. Distillates are still the main support for refining margins, per a Kpler note headline. Possible knock-on: crude that can't be refined at home may be pushed into exports, if ports are intact. The reports give no damage or restart timelines. Plant-level outage reporting in the coming days will show how long the capacity stays offline.

The first Russian diesel cargo bound for the US has stopped off the US east coast. Its operator says no destination has been decided [Reuters via gCaptain]. The US energy secretary says he is confident the cargo will eventually be delivered [Reuters via gCaptain].

## Peace bets sink Brent sub-$90

Front-month Brent hit $89.70 early Tuesday in Europe, its first move below $90 since August. WTI traded below $85 [OilPrice.com]. At the Asian open Brent was $91.61 and WTI $85.81 [OilPrice.com]. Crude is down almost $30/bbl since the midterms [OilPrice.com].

The trigger was President Trump saying he is keen to pursue diplomacy and wind down the war, after meeting Qatar's prime minister [Rigzone]. He also signalled no strikes on Iran for now. Reports that Washington would let Iran enrich uranium to almost 4% have not been confirmed by official sources [OilPrice.com].

Physical flows are recovering faster than costs are falling:

- **Gulf exports:** vessel tracking has shown Gulf flows close to pre-war levels for over a month, and Middle East benchmarks are weakening [OilPrice.com].
- **Freight and insurance:** war cover, insurance and record tanker rates still keep refiners' delivered costs high [OilPrice.com]. VLCC rates from the Gulf to China have dipped only marginally [OilPrice.com].
- **Chinese buying:** China's October crude imports were 41.94m t, about 9.88m b/d. That is the highest since March and up 9.4% on September, but 13.3% below October 2025 [customs via Reuters via OilPrice.com].
- **US pump prices:** regular gasoline averaged $3.9788/gal on Tuesday, the first reading below $4 since July [AAA via OilPrice.com].

On US stocks, API estimates a 9.0m bbl crude build in the week to 6 November, after a 5.267m bbl draw the week before. Another 2.7m bbl left the SPR [API via OilPrice.com]. API also reports a distillate build, but the item gives no figure.

## ADNOC vessel hit in UAE waters

**Confirmed attacks:**
- An ADNOC Logistics & Services vessel was damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters on Tuesday [gCaptain; World Oil].
- UKMTO separately reported an apparent projectile strike on a tanker near Hormuz [UKMTO via gCaptain].
- Production at the Khor Mor gas facility in Iraqi Kurdistan is still suspended after a suspected drone attack and fire on 8 November. Damage is still being assessed [Dana Gas/Crescent via World Oil].

**Claimed only:** The Houthis say they hit East-West pipeline pumping stations, Abqaiq and Riyadh's King Khalid airport. Riyadh has not acknowledged the attacks [Houthi statement via AP via OilPrice.com]. The pipeline feeds Yanbu, Saudi Arabia's Hormuz bypass outlet.

**Efforts to keep tankers moving:**
- Bahri says it is sending tankers through Hormuz in coordination with the US military [Bahri via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com].
- President Macron is discussing a publicly backed vehicle with Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states to take on part of the war-risk insurance for Hormuz transits [Rigzone]. Gulf states have not commented.

## Qatari LNG back to half pre-war

Qatar's LNG loadings have picked up over recent weeks to around half of pre-war levels [Reuters via gCaptain]. Ras Laffan is still under force majeure.

- **Hormuz LNG transits:** 31 LNG tankers crossed in October, against 27 in September. That is the most since February, and most cargoes loaded at Ras Laffan [Kpler via Reuters via OilPrice.com].
- **Ship-to-ship transfers:** two of those cargoes were moved to other tankers after clearing the strait [Kpler via Reuters via OilPrice.com].
- **EU storage:** 72.0% full on 9 November, down 0.39 points on the day [GIE AGSI].
- **US gas:** the EIA now expects almost 70 bcf more gas in US storage at end-November than it forecast in October, after a warm start to the month [EIA].
