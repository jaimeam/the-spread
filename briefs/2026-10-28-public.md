# The Spread - Wednesday, 28 October 2026

Saudi Arabia contained a fire near Ras Tanura, its biggest export terminal, after a Houthi missile and drone attack, and Brent firmed in early Asian trade. Aramco is also offering discounted spot crude to Europe from its Red Sea and Mediterranean outlets.

## Ras Tanura fire contained

Saudi Arabia said early Tuesday it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Region and four drones aimed at the south. A fire then broke out near the Ras Tanura export hub and was quickly contained, according to an unnamed source [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. The Houthis say a ballistic missile hit the Ras Tanura refinery [gCaptain]. Riyadh has not confirmed a hit on the refinery. The site sits on the Gulf side, so any outage would fall on loadings that already depend on Hormuz transits and STS transfers. Damage to the terminal and refinery isn't reported. An Aramco or energy ministry statement, or loading data for the next few days, will show whether exports were affected.

Brent traded at $115.70 early Wednesday, up 1.62%, with WTI at $101.20, up 1.34% [OilPrice.com]. That followed a fall of nearly $2 on Tuesday, which Rigzone put down to signs of Gulf de-escalation diplomacy [Rigzone]. Reports that Washington is preparing a new campaign against Iran after the midterms pushed the other way [OilPrice.com]. Both rest on reports, not confirmed plans.

## Russia swaps crude for diesel

Russian crude exports from Baltic and Black Sea ports could fall by 100,000-200,000 b/d in November from October, according to two traders working from preliminary loading schedules [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. The figures include Kazakh crude piped through Russia. The cause is refiners running harder for diesel under the 9 October deal with Washington. Western buyers of Urals get fewer barrels, and more Russian diesel becomes available for export. Russia's seaborne crude shipments are already at their lowest in more than a month, even though the key Baltic port has recovered from its halt [gCaptain]. Analysts still expect the diesel shortage to last into 2027 and say the US-Russia deal won't change that [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone reports that new Indian refining capacity will lift diesel exports next year [Rigzone].

## Aramco discounts crude to Europe

Aramco is offering European refiners extra crude for October and November loading, according to a person familiar with the matter. Late-October spot cargoes are priced below the official selling price and load at Yanbu on the Red Sea or Sidi Kerir on Egypt's Mediterranean coast [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Only weeks earlier, Aramco had told customers they would get no oil under October contracts after the East-West pipeline was attacked. Supplying from these outlets keeps the cargoes clear of Hormuz, and European refiners get medium-sour crude below OSP. Possible knock-on: these barrels compete with other Atlantic Basin sour grades for European runs. Aramco is also close to a $10bn bank loan [Rigzone].

## Iran threatens Sinokor tankers

Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority says it will take legal action against tankers owned by Sinokor [gCaptain]. Sinokor is the world's biggest tanker owner and has carried millions of barrels out of the strait in recent weeks. The threat is Iran's statement alone, and no action has been reported. More than 30 incidents have been recorded in and around Hormuz in October, against 14 in September [OilPrice.com]. The latest target was a crude tanker in Iraq's exclusive economic zone. Evercore ISI doubled its 2027 VLCC earnings forecast from $57,500 [Splash247].

## OPEC+ seen holding December quotas

OPEC+ is expected to leave December quotas unchanged when it meets on Sunday, sources say [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. The 1.65m b/d rollback finished in September [OilPrice.com], but the war has made targets largely notional for eight months, so physical flows matter more than the decision.

## India squeezed by $123 basket

India's crude basket reached $123.29/bbl last week [local media via OilPrice.com]. Together with a weak rupee, that left fuel marketers losing money even after pump price rises. Oil Minister Puri denied US pressure to cut Russian crude imports and said purchases follow price [OilPrice.com]. He spoke as Russian arrivals decline and Middle East imports rise. Fuel export earnings reached $27.2bn from April to September, up 50% in rupee terms [oil ministry via OilPrice.com].
