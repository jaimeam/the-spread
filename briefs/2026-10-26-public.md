# The Spread - Monday, 26 October 2026

Brent ended last week above $116 after more attacks on Saudi Arabia, even though Middle East crude exports are now running above pre-war levels. Freight is the other squeeze: tanker costs are pricing US crude out of Asia and lifting fuel oil above crude in Singapore.

## $116 Brent despite Gulf flows

Brent topped $116 on Friday as attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated [Rigzone], after touching $114 intraday that morning [Rigzone]. OilPrice.com puts crude above $117 by Friday evening [OilPrice.com]. The rally came as October Middle East oil exports ran higher than before the US and Israeli strikes on Iran at the end of February [Kpler via OilPrice.com].

The physical market is not following futures. Prices for some Middle East crudes sold outside the Gulf fell on Friday [Rigzone]. The premium in Brent is pricing the risk of losing Saudi barrels, not a current shortage. Refiners buying on futures-linked formulas pay that premium even though cargoes are available.

Today's items do not say which Saudi sites were hit on Friday or whether output was affected. A statement from Aramco or the Saudi energy ministry would settle it.

Separately, Ras Al Khaimah closed all its beaches on Saturday after an offshore oil leak the day before. The source of the leak is not given [Reuters via gCaptain].

## Freight walls off US crude

Goldman Sachs puts the global average crude freight rate above $20/bbl, against $3/bbl a year ago [Goldman Sachs via Rigzone]. On the long-haul route, a US Gulf Coast to China voyage cost as much as $80m earlier this month, about ten times the pre-war norm, and was still about $60m after easing [OilPrice.com].

At those rates there is little incentive to ship US crude to Asia. US crude exports to Asia are on course for multi-year lows, possibly the lowest in seven years [gCaptain]. That leaves more light sweet crude in the Atlantic Basin, while Asian refiners lean on nearer supply.

Freight is also showing up in product prices. Singapore high-sulphur fuel oil now trades above the crude it is made from [OilPrice.com]. The causes are lost Middle East and Russian supply, steady bunker demand and the cost of shipping replacement cargoes. More Gulf supply in the season and extra Chinese export quotas could ease very-low-sulphur fuel oil towards year-end [OilPrice.com].

## SPR exchange for Lake Charles

On Friday the DOE authorised an emergency exchange of up to 400,000 bbl from the West Hackberry site to Phillips 66's Lake Charles refinery. It cited a temporary disruption to crude deliveries and gave no cause [DOE via OilPrice.com]. The reserve is at its lowest since 1982 [OilPrice.com]. The exchange keeps one Gulf Coast plant running; it does not add product supply.

## Russian diesel due in Norfolk 2 November

A tanker carrying Russian diesel is expected in Norfolk, Virginia, on 2 November, with a second cargo heading for New York [gCaptain]. That answers last week's open question on whether the first cargo had sailed. It would be the first Russian diesel to reach the US in more than three years [gCaptain]. Volumes are not reported.

In Europe, diesel and jet supply remains tight. The European Commission says there is no physical shortage [European Commission via Rigzone]. Germany says it will try to stop further refinery closures, with no measures detailed [Rigzone].

## EU gas storage stalls at 74.6%

EU storage was 74.6% full on 24 October, down 0.01 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. That is the same level as on 18 October, so net injections have been flat for about a week heading into winter.

Britain is expected to rely more heavily on gas imports this winter [Rigzone]. UK proven and probable reserves fell to 2.5 Bboe at end-2025, down 0.4 Bboe, with no exploration wells drilled that year [NSTA via World Oil].
