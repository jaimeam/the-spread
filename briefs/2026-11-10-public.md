# The Spread - Tuesday, 10 November 2026

Qatar's prime minister is taking an Iranian interim proposal to reopen Hormuz to the White House, and Brent fell about 7% on Monday. That happened even though Saudi Arabia's main Hormuz bypass pipeline was attacked the same day.

## Qatar takes Iran offer to US

Brent fell 3.6% to $94.00/bbl and WTI fell 3% to $88.09 by early Monday, extending last week's 16% slide [OilPrice.com]. By the close the fall had reached about 7% [OilPrice.com]. The Qatari prime minister was heading to Washington with Iranian terms for an interim deal to reopen the strait. Two diplomatic sources told Israeli media that Tehran has signed off on them [OilPrice.com]. Separately, Al Arabiya cited unnamed sources saying Washington would let Iran enrich uranium to 3.67% under a Qatari-Pakistani mediation track [OilPrice.com].

All of this is one-sided or unsourced. Neither government has confirmed terms. Trump said only that further strikes depend on a very strong agreement, signalling no new attacks for now [OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. Rubio called Tehran's demands so far unacceptable [Rigzone].

The market is pricing the Hormuz supply-risk premium out of crude ahead of any physical change in flows. Washington's response isn't in today's reports; the outcome of the Qatari PM's White House visit will settle it.

Flows are still constrained in the meantime. EIA data show US imports of Iraqi crude at their lowest since the 2003 war, falling to zero in several summer weeks [EIA via OilPrice.com]. Iraq's foreign minister puts war losses at about $60bn [Al Jazeera via OilPrice.com].

## Saudi bypass pipeline hit

The East-West pipeline was attacked in the Medina region on Monday. An Energy Ministry official said the attack caused a limited fire, which was extinguished, with no casualties and no disruption to operations [Saudi Energy Ministry via OilPrice.com]. The line feeds Yanbu on the Red Sea, which handled most of the kingdom's export growth in October.

The official's account means the Hormuz workaround is still running. The attack nonetheless puts the main bypass route directly in the firing line.

A Houthi attack also damaged an Aramco tower in Riyadh's financial district [Rigzone]. A Reuters survey put OPEC output back at 90% of its pre-war rate [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

In the strait itself, tankers were set ablaze over the weekend and the US military bombed a bulk carrier on Saturday [Splash247]. Iran issued fresh mine warnings, and explosions were reported along its southern coast [Splash247]. Attribution beyond the US action is not reported.

## Russian diesel cargoes divert from US

Two tankers carrying Russian diesel, Talisman and Leruo, have diverted from their declared US destinations after loading at Primorsk in October [OilPrice.com]. A third is in the North Atlantic with no declared discharge port.

Treasury has authorised Russian diesel transactions under Trump's arrangement with Putin. Lawmakers, however, warn that importers could still breach a congressional ban. The legal risk to buyers is keeping these barrels from landing, so this route offers no relief yet to US distillate stocks, which stood at 99,259 kb in the week to 30 October [EIA].

## LNG above $30 as China steps back

LNG is trading above $30/MMBtu going into winter, and Europe holds its lowest gas inventory of the 2020s [OilPrice.com]. EU storage was 72.4% full on 8 November, down 0.28 points on the day [GIE AGSI].

China is cutting spot purchases as cheap domestic supply and inventory releases kick in. China's national average LNG price is $21.97/MMBtu, almost $10 below JKM [Bloomberg via OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: weaker Chinese bidding could leave more spot cargoes for Europe.

Still, cloudy weather drove European peak power prices to 2022 highs on Monday, lifting gas burn [OilPrice.com]. Low wind is expected to last until at least midweek.
