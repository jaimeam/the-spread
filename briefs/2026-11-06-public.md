# The Spread - Friday, 6 November 2026

Crude recovered about 4% on Thursday as traders doubted that the midterm result means a quick end to the Iran war, and Tehran said it is exempting China and India from its Hormuz restrictions. In the US, refiners are making less gasoline than in any recent year so they can produce more diesel and jet fuel.

## Crude rebounds 4% after rout

Brent was up 4.0% at $98.38/bbl at 3:15pm ET Thursday, and WTI up 2.9% at $90.65 [OilPrice.com]. It was the first rise in four sessions and followed Wednesday's settle of $94.62 [OilPrice.com]. The selling ran into Thursday morning, with Brent at $94.30 before Europe opened [OilPrice.com], and then reversed. Traders were reconsidering whether a Democratic Congress can actually shorten the war while Trump stays in office [OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. European gas followed the same path. Front-month TTF was down 2.2% early Thursday on hopes that Qatari LNG would return through Hormuz [OilPrice.com], then closed the day up 1.9% at €89.72/MWh [OilPrice.com]. Henry Hub was flat at $2.95/MMBtu [OilPrice.com].

The risk premium is moving on political signals rather than on any change in flows. Gulf exporters, Asian buyers and European gas importers all feel it. No ceasefire terms or talks schedule appear in today's reports, so a published framework from Washington or Tehran is what would settle the direction.

## Iran exempts China and India

Iran's parliament says it is exempting several key trading partners, including China and India, from its oil transit restrictions in Hormuz. Ahmad Hayat-Moghaddam, vice chair of the parliament's national security committee, announced the move on Tuesday [OilPrice.com]. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues. This is Iran's own announcement: no vessel movements under the exemption are reported, and neither Beijing nor New Delhi has commented.

On the same day, the IRGC said two oil tankers and a tug caught fire in the strait. It said one tanker hit a mine while heading south, and the tug caught fire after going to help the second tanker [IRGC via OilPrice.com]. Only Iran has reported these incidents; no owner, flag state or UKMTO confirmation has appeared.

Possible knock-on: if the exemption holds, Chinese and Indian refiners could regain some Gulf barrels while other buyers stay squeezed on freight and insurance.

## US refiners swap gasoline for diesel

Gasoline averaged below 45% of US refinery output in January-August 2026, beneath the previous five-year low in every month. The usual range is 45-50% [EIA via OilPrice.com]. The EIA links the shift to unusually strong margins on distillate and jet fuel, using Petroleum Supply Monthly data through August [EIA].

Refiners are running hard as they make the switch. Utilisation was 94.5% in the week to 30 October, up 1.7 points, and distillate stocks were 99,259 kb [EIA]. More middle distillates from USGC refiners eases the US diesel balance and leaves gasoline relatively short.

## Chinese diesel exports to drop

Chinese refiners have sold about 500,000 t of diesel for November loading so far, and exports are set to fall from the August-September highs [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. The reason given is that those late-summer shipments drew domestic inventories low. Deals for second-half November loading are not yet finalised [Reuters via OilPrice.com]. Less Chinese diesel in Asia supports regional middle-distillate cracks. Possible knock-on: Middle East and Indian cargoes get pulled east, leaving less for Europe.

Sources: [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Chinas-Diesel-Exports-Set-to-Plunge-as-Domestic-Inventories-Run-Low.html)

## EU gas draw steepens

EU storage was 73.8% full on 4 November, down 0.32 points in a day [GIE AGSI]. It was 74.1% on 3 November [GIE AGSI].

Unnamed EU diplomats told EUobserver that the bloc has no plans to resume buying Russian gas. They spoke ahead of next week's US-Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi [EUobserver via OilPrice.com]. That leaves Europe's winter balance dependent on LNG.

Separately, Rhine water levels are forecast to fall again in the coming days, which raises the risk of higher barge rates for inland German and Swiss supply [Rigzone].
