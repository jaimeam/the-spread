# The Spread - Thursday, 12 November 2026

Gulf crude exports are back near four-fifths of pre-war levels, but refined products are lagging badly, and US diesel futures jumped as Iran denied talks with Washington. Separately, Poland's Orlen became the first buyer to walk away from a long-term Qatari LNG contract.

## Gulf products stuck at 35%

Middle East crude exports, including pipeline barrels, have recovered to about 80-85% of pre-war levels, while refined-product exports remain at about 35% [FGE via OilPrice.com]. Producers are moving crude first and holding back products while the strait stays vulnerable. That keeps diesel and jet from Jubail, Ruwais and Al-Zour scarce even as crude loosens. The split showed in prices on Wednesday: heating oil, the US diesel benchmark, rose 4.1%, outpacing Brent's gain of nearly 3% [OilPrice.com]. Refiners with crude access gain margin, and product importers who relied on Gulf cargoes pay more.

Crude supply is clearly recovering. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it produced 10.563m b/d in October, up 26% from 8.372m b/d in September. That is the highest since February and slightly above its quota of about 10.5m b/d [Saudi data via Bloomberg via OilPrice.com; Rigzone]. Riyadh put supply to the market, net of storage, at 9.203m b/d [Saudi data via OilPrice.com].

Possible knock-on: if crude keeps outrunning products, crude-heavy cracks could widen further, especially for distillates.

## Orlen cancels Qatar LNG deal

Orlen terminated its long-term contract with QatarEnergy for 1m t a year, citing the supplier's inability to deliver under force majeure, according to a regulatory filing late Tuesday [Orlen via OilPrice.com; gCaptain]. The deal was signed in 2009, with deliveries from 2016 and a term to 2034 [The National via OilPrice.com]. It is the first long-term Qatari customer to cancel. A QatarEnergy response isn't reported.

Europe's gas balance stays tight around it. EU storage stood at 71.6% on 10 November, down 0.39 points on the day [GIE AGSI]. Front-month TTF rose 2.7% to about €87/MWh on Wednesday, recovering from a one-month low near €82 earlier in the week [OilPrice.com]. In a 4 November note, Kpler expected TTF to head back toward €100/MWh on colder weather, lower Norwegian flows and early withdrawals [Kpler]. Germany will go ahead with a 24 TWh state crisis gas reserve funded by user surcharges, after missing its winter storage targets [German economy ministry via gCaptain].

Possible knock-on: if other long-term buyers follow Orlen, Qatari volumes freed later could reach the spot market rather than legacy contracts.

## Crude rebounds as Tehran denies talks

Brent was up nearly 3% at $93.90/bbl at 11:18 ET Wednesday, with WTI at $87.98 [OilPrice.com]. The move followed a denial by Mohsen Rezaei, Iran's top security official, that Tehran is negotiating with Washington [OilPrice.com]. This is Iran's position only. The reported Qatari mediation track remains unconfirmed by either side. Brent had lost about 21% since the start of November on bets the war was ending [OilPrice.com]. As of a 5 November note, trend-following funds had sold around 126m bbl during the slide [Kpler].

Attacks continued. UKMTO reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile 19nm northeast of the strait on Tuesday, one of two tankers attacked that day [UKMTO via Splash247]. Iran claims three vessels hit naval mines; owners and flag states have not confirmed this [Splash247]. As many as 37 vessels were attacked in October, a monthly record [OilPrice.com].

## Drones hit Ust-Luga export hub

Ukrainian drones struck the port area of Ust-Luga, Russia's Baltic export hub, early Wednesday, according to the Leningrad regional governor [Leningrad governor via OilPrice.com]. The strike started a fire, injured three workers and forced the emergency shutdown of a transformer substation [Splash247]. The strike moves Ukraine's campaign from refineries to export infrastructure. It came during a second day of US-led Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi [Rigzone]. The effect on loadings isn't in today's reports; port and tanker-tracking data will show whether Baltic crude and product exports were interrupted.

Separately, the Russian diesel tanker that had idled off the US East Coast made a U-turn about 400nm off New York [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. Analysts say US buyers are unlikely to take Russian diesel despite Trump's temporary sanctions waiver [gCaptain].

## Canada re-sanctions Iran's tanker company

Canada reinstated sanctions on the National Iranian Tanker Company, reversing relief granted under the nuclear deal. The package covers 11 entities, including NIOC and Naftiran Intertrade [Splash247]. In a 2 November note, Kpler said higher-risk tankers are lifting a growing share of Gulf crude [Kpler].
