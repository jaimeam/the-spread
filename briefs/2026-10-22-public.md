# The Spread - Thursday, 22 October 2026

Two more tankers were hit in the Gulf and a drone killed two people at Saudi Arabia's Shaybah field, yet Middle East crude is still moving close to pre-war volumes. Freight, not supply, is now the binding constraint, both for Gulf barrels to Asia and for US diesel to Europe.

## Hormuz: two more tankers hit as Gulf crude keeps flowing

Two tankers were reported struck by unidentified projectiles on Wednesday. One was near the strait and one in the northern Persian Gulf, a location further from the earlier cluster [gCaptain]. A separate tally logs at least 23 incidents in or near Hormuz in the first two weeks of October, at least five with engine-room damage [Rigzone]. That is a fuller count for the same period as earlier tallies, not evidence of a faster pace.

Physical flows tell a different story. Middle East shipments show little sign of buckling [Bloomberg via gCaptain]. The Oman-Dubai premium, the main gauge of Gulf sour tightness, has fallen to $4.22/bbl, its lowest since end-July [General Index via OilPrice.com]. It peaked at $65 in March, when Hormuz was virtually closed [OilPrice.com]. Saudi August exports rose 7.9% m/m to 4.451m b/d, the highest since March, even as production hit a record low of 6.24m b/d [JODI via OilPrice.com]. Saudi refinery runs fell to 2.297m b/d [JODI via OilPrice.com].

China is a large part of the pull. In a 13 October note, Kpler said Chinese buyers had secured at least 80m bbl of Saudi crude since mid-September, as onshore stocks fell a record ~1.6m b/d in September [Kpler].

The cost has moved to freight. Shipping crude from the Gulf to Asia now costs more than $37/bbl, over five times pre-war [IEA via gCaptain]. Sour barrels are available, but Asian refiners are paying for them in landed cost rather than in the benchmark.

Indian Oil Corp will trim its Middle East term share for FY2027/28 below the planned 47%. That comes from one anonymous source, and IOC has not confirmed it [Reuters via OilPrice.com].

## Shaybah drone strike kills two, output unaffected

A drone hit Aramco's 1m b/d Shaybah field, killing two and injuring two. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry says operations were unaffected [World Oil]. The items do not name an attacker. Crude rose on the attack and on Gulf embassy concerns, but no settlement figure is reported [Rigzone].

## US diesel: freight shuts the Europe arb as stocks build

The EIA weekly for the week to 16 October showed the following [EIA]:
- Commercial crude fell 3,060 kb to 412,517 kb.
- Gasoline fell 2,952 kb to 205,310 kb.
- Distillate rose 1,675 kb to 104,286 kb.
- Refinery utilisation was 93.4%.

Crude is now 3% below the five-year average [EIA via OilPrice.com].

The distillate build fits Kpler's 20 October read. TC14 freight near 400 Worldscale, not price, has closed the US Gulf diesel arbitrage to Europe, and physical differentials swung $30/t in one week [Kpler]. With freight that high, USGC barrels stay home, and Europe loses its swing diesel supplier. Possible knock-on: firmer NWE diesel cracks if TC14 stays elevated.

Washington is also tapping the Northeast home heating oil reserve; no volume is given [Rigzone]. Novak says the first Russian diesel cargo for the US sails today under the Putin-Trump deal. That is Moscow's account; no US confirmation or volume is in the items [gCaptain].

## Iran sanctions: new designations alongside unpublished licences

OFAC designated a law firm, shipping agents and nine vessels accused of moving Iranian oil [gCaptain]. On the same day it issued Iran- and Russia-related general licences [OFAC]. Their terms are not in today's items, so it is unclear whether they are the shadow-fleet relief Wright described.

Kpler's 14 October estimate was that only about 24 of 160 OFAC-sanctioned VLCCs could realistically rejoin trade, too few to ease record freight [Kpler].

## European gas: storage dips, Jackdaw approved

EU storage was 74.6% full on 20 October, down 0.04 points d/d [GIE AGSI]. It is the first daily fall after weeks of small injections.

The UK approved the Jackdaw field. First gas could come by end-October, lifting UK output about 6% at peak [World Oil; UK DESNZ]. Macron says France will receive Qatari LNG this winter. The items carry no volumes and no Qatari confirmation [Rigzone].
