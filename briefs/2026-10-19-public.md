# The Spread - Monday, 19 October 2026

Brent touched $110 for the first time since May as Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Russia-Ukraine energy attacks added risk premium. Over the weekend, Iran said two tankers hit mines in Hormuz and repeated its offer to reopen the strait.

## Brent reaches $110 on Houthi and Russia-Ukraine strikes

Brent rose 3.7% to $110/bbl, its first visit to that level since May [World Oil; Rigzone]. Both reports attribute the move to Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Russia-Ukraine energy attacks, not to any new loss of physical supply.

The curve shows the market expects tightness to last. As of Saturday, January 2027 Brent traded at $102.59 and June 2027 at $92.71 [OilPrice.com]. That steep backwardation rewards prompt barrels and penalises storage.

## Hormuz: Iran says two tankers mined, repeats reopening offer

On Saturday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines while crossing the strait [IRGC via gCaptain]. Only the Iranian side has reported this. The ships are not named, and no shipowner, UKMTO or CENTCOM confirmation is in today's items.

On Sunday, Foreign Minister Araqchi said Iran's offer to reopen Hormuz within seven days is still on the table [Araqchi via gCaptain]. Washington has not accepted it, and the terms are not reported.

The picture on the water is getting harder to read. UKMTO says most ship attacks go unreported, and traffic counts conflict [UKMTO via gCaptain]. That undercuts last week's CENTCOM claim of US control of the strait.

Freight is now changing how crude is priced. Delivered costs are running above what refiners will pay, so sellers are:
- cutting differentials,
- seeking nearer buyers, or
- selling forward on the bet that freight eases [OilPrice.com].

Chinese refiners are reported to be waiting for freight to fall. Bunker supply is also strained: fuel is available, but finding the right grade at the right port is getting harder [OilPrice.com]. Possible knock-on: if Chinese buyers keep waiting, Gulf and Atlantic sellers will have to widen discounts further.

Whether mines are now in the shipping lanes isn't confirmed by any non-Iranian source. A UKMTO advisory or shipowner statement would settle it.

## Stock releases: IEA commits the last 100m bbl, US offers 17m more

IEA members agreed on Friday to release the 100m bbl still outstanding from the March intervention [IEA via Rigzone]. This completes the existing pledge. It is not the extra 100m bbl the US was reported to be seeking, which remains unconfirmed.

The same day, the DOE issued a request for proposals to exchange up to 17m bbl of SPR crude [DOE via OilPrice.com]. The barrels count toward the 172m bbl US share of the IEA action. The SPR holds about 283m bbl [DOE via OilPrice.com].

These are crude barrels. They ease prompt crude tightness but do little directly for the diesel shortage.

## Russian diesel deal gains a buyer: Turkey

Turkish Energy Minister Bayraktar said Turkey will take some of the diesel Russia is supplying under its deal with Trump [Bayraktar via gCaptain]. Turkey is the first named destination in today's items. No volumes or timing are given.

Turkish demand absorbing Russian barrels would leave less for Europe to compete over. Today's reports do not show any Russian cargoes moving yet.

In the Black Sea, Romanian F-16s shot down two drones over Romanian territorial waters on Saturday [Romanian defence ministry via gCaptain]. Separately, Ukraine is in talks with Turkey, Egypt and India on reopening civil navigation [Bloomberg via gCaptain].

## Gas: EU storage gains pace; Scarborough LNG paused

EU storage rose to 74.5% full on 17 October, up 0.16 points d/d [GIE AGSI]. That compares with 74.1% on 14 October. The daily build is quicker than last week's 0.08-0.10 point gains, but storage is still low going into winter.

The EU is estimated to have paid an extra €100bn for oil and gas so far this year [OilPrice.com]. Ministers are looking at ways to cushion prices.

Woodside paused pre-commissioning at the floating production unit for its $12.5bn Scarborough project after Greenpeace protesters boarded the platform [Woodside via gCaptain; World Oil]. The pause was still in place on Sunday, and first LNG had been due in Q4. China is reported to have stockpiled enough gas for winter, according to analysts [Rigzone], which would ease competition with Europe for spot LNG.
