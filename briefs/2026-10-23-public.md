# The Spread - Friday, 23 October 2026

Oil jumped about 4% after deadly attacks in Saudi Arabia, and Iran claimed seven more vessels hit in the Gulf, four of them mid-transfer. Freight is now the binding constraint on both crude and diesel, shutting US diesel exports to Europe.

## Oil jumps 4% on Saudi attacks

December Brent traded at $111.70 on Thursday, up $2.88, after touching $112.68 [OilPrice.com]. Rigzone has Brent above $113 with a 4% gain [Rigzone]. The two reports likely reflect different times of day.

Rigzone ties the move to deadly attacks in Saudi Arabia, renewed Iran tension and tight diesel. Aramco evacuated its headquarters as Houthi attacks on the kingdom continue [Rigzone]. Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said Riyadh is in talks with foreign governments to give Aramco access to their strategic storage, so it can keep supplying customers during disruptions [Aramco via gCaptain]. No partners or volumes are named.

Citi has moved from forecasting $60-65 Brent by year-end, three months ago, to seeing more upside risk [Citi via OilPrice.com]. Its argument is that Gulf crude is getting out, but not cheaply. More than 1m b/d of Gulf crude has moved between vessels in ship-to-ship (STS) transfers [Citi via OilPrice.com]. That bypass route is what Iran now says it is targeting.

## Iran claims seven ships hit

The IRGC Navy says seven vessels were attacked or struck mines across the Gulf and Hormuz in 24 hours [IRGC via Splash247]. It says four of them were hit together while transferring oil, after using a route Tehran calls unauthorised [IRGC via Splash247].

This is Iran's claim alone. Trump accused Tehran of faking hits [gCaptain]. No owner, flag state or navy has confirmed any of the seven.

If the STS strikes are real, they hit the same transfers Citi counts at more than 1m b/d [Citi via OilPrice.com]. That would raise war-risk cover and tie up more tonnage in an already tight tanker market. Independent confirmation of vessel names or damage is still missing; owner statements or UKMTO notices would settle it.

## Freight shuts US diesel out of Europe

Kpler's 18 October note puts a figure on the closed arbitrage (the price gap that makes shipping diesel worthwhile). Medium-range (MR) tanker freight rose about 30% week on week. That pushed the US Gulf Coast-to-Rotterdam diesel arb to -$22.25/t [Kpler]. The same freight move shut Indian diesel out of the Mediterranean [Kpler].

Europe's two main swing diesel suppliers are both priced out at once. US refiners are already near the limit: utilisation averaged more than 95% from May to September, the highest for those months since 1998, driven by diesel margins [EIA]. US distillate stocks rose 1,675 kb to 104,286 kb in the week to 16 October [EIA]. Possible knock-on: barrels that cannot reach Europe build up in US storage while European diesel cracks firm.

Sources: [Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/us-reopening-quickly-fades-as-freight-climbs) · [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=68344)

## Qatar signs to buy US LNG

QatarEnergy signed two long-term deals to buy up to 4m t/y of US LNG from Cheniere through 2054 [gCaptain]. It is a Gulf exporter hedging its own customer book while Hormuz constrains it.

Splash247 describes Gulf LNG exports as near-paralysed. Cargoes are being redirected, Asian buyers are turning to other suppliers, and gas prices are approaching $30/MMBtu [Splash247]. EU storage was 74.6% full on 21 October, up 0.02 points [GIE AGSI].

Sources: [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/qatarenergy-signs-long-term-deal-to-buy-u-s-lng-as-iran-war-disrupts-exports/) · [Splash247](https://splash247.com/a-new-world-order-emerges-in-lng-shipping/)

## China's buffers thin out

China's petrochemical glut, built up over years of overproduction, has largely been used up as the war chokes feedstock flows [Rigzone]. On crude, China drew on its 1.3bn bbl of stocks for most months after March and cut imports [OilPrice.com]. Rising imports now mean it needs to draw less [OilPrice.com].

That draw-down was one of the market's main buffers. Possible knock-on: if China is buying rather than drawing, it competes with other buyers for scarce seaborne crude, though the reports give no import volumes.

Sources: [Rigzone](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/chinas_petrochemicals_glut_turns_to_crunch-22-oct-2026-184910-article/?rss=true) · [OilPrice.com](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Rising-Crude-Oil-Imports-Reduce-Chinas-Need-to-Draw-from-Stocks.html)
