This is The Spread, your energy markets briefing for Thursday, the twelfth of November. This episode is written and read with AI.

Gulf crude exports are back near four-fifths of pre-war levels, but refined products are lagging badly, and US diesel futures jumped as Iran denied holding talks with Washington. In Europe, Poland's Orlen has become the first buyer to walk away from a long-term Qatari gas contract.

On the markets. Brent was up nearly three percent at ninety-three dollars ninety a barrel late on Wednesday morning in New York, according to OilPrice dot com. West Texas Intermediate was near eighty-eight dollars, and heating oil, the US diesel benchmark, rose four point one percent. In Europe, front-month gas at the Dutch Title Transfer Facility, or TTF, rose two point seven percent to about eighty-seven euros a megawatt hour. EU gas storage stood at seventy-one point six percent on the tenth of November, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe.

The Gulf's recovery is lopsided. According to FGE, Middle East crude exports, pipeline barrels included, are back to about eighty to eighty-five percent of pre-war levels. Refined products are stuck at around thirty-five percent.

Producers are moving crude first and holding back products while the strait stays vulnerable. That keeps diesel and jet fuel from Jubail, Ruwais and Al-Zour scarce, even as crude loosens. It's why heating oil outran Brent on Wednesday. Refiners with access to crude pick up margin, while importers who leaned on Gulf product cargoes are paying more.

The crude side is clearly coming back. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it produced just under ten point six million barrels a day in October. That's up twenty-six percent from September.

It's the kingdom's highest output since February, and slightly above its quota of about ten and a half million. Riyadh put supply to the market, net of storage, at about nine point two million barrels a day. If crude keeps outrunning products, diesel margins could widen further.

Turning to gas. Orlen has terminated its long-term contract with QatarEnergy for one million tonnes of liquefied natural gas, or LNG, a year. In a regulatory filing late on Tuesday, the Polish company cited the supplier's inability to deliver under force majeure.

The deal dates from two thousand nine. Deliveries began in twenty sixteen and were due to run to twenty thirty-four. Orlen is the first long-term Qatari customer to cancel, and there's no reported response yet from QatarEnergy.

It lands in a tight European market. Storage slipped about four tenths of a point on the day, and Wednesday's TTF gain came off a one-month low near eighty-two euros earlier in the week. In a note dated the fourth of November, Kpler said it expects TTF to head back toward one hundred euros, citing colder weather, lower Norwegian flows and early withdrawals.

Germany, meanwhile, will go ahead with a state crisis gas reserve of twenty-four terawatt hours after missing its winter storage targets, according to the economy ministry. Gas users will fund it through surcharges. Should other long-term buyers follow Orlen, the Qatari volumes freed up later could end up on the spot market rather than in legacy contracts.

Back to the Gulf, and the politics behind Wednesday's oil rebound. The move followed a denial by Mohsen Rezaei, Iran's top security official, that Tehran is negotiating with Washington, OilPrice dot com reported. That is Iran's position only. The reported Qatari mediation track remains unconfirmed by either side.

Brent had lost about twenty-one percent since the start of November on bets the war was ending. As of a note on the fifth of November, Kpler estimated that trend-following funds had sold around one hundred and twenty-six million barrels during the slide.

The attacks haven't stopped. United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile on Tuesday, nineteen nautical miles northeast of the strait. It was one of two tankers attacked that day.

Iran claims three vessels hit naval mines, Splash twenty-four seven reported, though owners and flag states have not confirmed it. As many as thirty-seven vessels were attacked in October, a monthly record, according to OilPrice dot com. For shipowners and crews, the risk on this route is still very live.

Over in the Baltic, Ukrainian drones struck the port area of Ust-Luga, Russia's Baltic export hub, early on Wednesday, according to the Leningrad regional governor. The strike started a fire, injured three workers and forced the emergency shutdown of a transformer substation.

That moves Ukraine's campaign from refineries to export infrastructure. It came on the second day of US-led Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi, Rigzone reported. Today's reports don't show yet whether Baltic loadings were interrupted.

Separately, the Russian diesel tanker that had been idling off the US East Coast made a U-turn about four hundred nautical miles off New York, Bloomberg reported. Analysts cited by gCaptain say US buyers are unlikely to take Russian diesel, despite President Trump's temporary sanctions waiver.

On sanctions, Canada has reinstated measures against the National Iranian Tanker Company, reversing relief granted under the nuclear deal, according to Splash twenty-four seven. The package covers eleven entities, including the National Iranian Oil Company and Naftiran Intertrade. Kpler said in a note on the second of November that higher-risk tankers are lifting a growing share of Gulf crude.

A few more things.

The Panama Canal has raised daily transits to thirty-four and restored the Neopanamax draft to fifty feet after a wet October, according to the canal authority. That eases routing from the US Gulf to Asia for LNG and liquefied petroleum gas.

ADNOC Gas, part of the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company, doubled its third-quarter net income from the previous quarter to about one point three billion dollars. It raised its profit guidance for this year to at least four billion. ADNOC's ten-year LNG deal with Gujarat is put at up to four million tonnes.

US retail gasoline has fallen below four dollars a gallon for the first time since August, according to GasBuddy.

And the Department of Energy has closed a four hundred and eighty million dollar loan for Arena Energy to redevelop twenty-two platforms on the US Gulf shelf. At peak, the project could add nearly thirty-nine thousand barrels a day.
