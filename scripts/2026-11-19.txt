Washington has widened its blockade of Iran to cover ship-to-ship transfers, and another tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz has left a crew member missing. Even so, crude fell about three percent on Wednesday, as US stocks built across crude, gasoline and distillates.

On the markets. Early Wednesday, Brent traded at ninety-two dollars fifty a barrel and West Texas Intermediate at eighty-four dollars seventy, before oil closed the day about three percent lower. According to the EIA, US commercial crude stocks rose three point four million barrels in the week to the thirteenth of November. In Europe, gas storage was seventy percent full on Tuesday, down two tenths of a point on the day.

Starting with the blockade. US Central Command issued an advisory on Wednesday saying that ships taking cargo at sea from vessels loaded in Iran will now be treated as trying to evade the US blockade of Iranian ports. Those ships could face action. Until now, enforcement covered the Iranian ports themselves.

This goes after the transshipment step that hides where a cargo came from. Any tanker joining those transfers now carries blockade risk. So do buyers further down the chain, mainly in China, if the cargoes reach them. The advisory doesn't say how many cargoes it would catch.

The strikes are running both ways. US officials say American aircraft hit a missile launch site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday, belonging to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The Guard Corps says it had first attacked a US military site in Bahrain. That claim comes only from Iran, and no damage has been reported. Gulf states have issued fresh warnings to residents about incoming projectiles.

In the strait itself, the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company's shipping arm, ADNOC Logistics and Services, confirmed one of its vessels was attacked while transiting Hormuz on Tuesday evening. One crew member is unaccounted for and eleven were injured. The rest were evacuated. The company hasn't named the vessel or the attacker.

Separately, the liquefied petroleum gas carrier Tara Gas was still burning and adrift off Musandam on Wednesday, according to a navigational warning. India's foreign ministry says twenty-four of its twenty-five crew are accounted for.

With supertankers being targeted, more crude is moving on smaller ships, mostly through ship-to-ship transfers. According to Vortexa, Suezmax and Aframax tankers carried two million barrels a day out of the strait in October. That's the most since the war began. Each of those ships carries less than half the load of a very large crude carrier, so the same exports tie up more hulls and more transfers. And those transfers are exactly what the new US advisory targets.

Back to the price move. Traders pointed to rising supply, a stronger dollar and weaker appetite for risk. The EIA weekly data added to that. Gasoline stocks rose five point two million barrels. Distillates added one point six million, a second straight weekly build, taking them to just under one hundred and five million barrels.

Refineries ran at ninety-five point seven percent of capacity, up half a point. Total commercial petroleum stocks rose nine point two million barrels. Crude stocks, for what it's worth, are still three percent below the five-year average.

Turning to refining, where diesel is doing the heavy lifting. That's well above the two point two million figure in earlier Reuters reporting. Kpler also expects Russian diesel exports to fall short of the planned two million tonnes.

With less Russian gasoil on the water, gasoil is leading a margin rally while gasoline and naphtha lag. Fund positioning points the other way, though. Kpler said in an earlier note, dated the tenth, that trend-following funds cut their Brent exposure from forty-five percent long to nine percent long in a single day. Their positions in gasoil and US gasoline futures turned net short for the first time in more than four months. Refiners with diesel-heavy output are the winners here. If the Russian shortfall lasts, the tight physical market and the short funds could pull further apart.
