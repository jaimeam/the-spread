France says Europe will release forty-two million barrels of emergency oil stocks over the next three months. Half of it is diesel, which makes this the most direct relief yet for record diesel prices. Meanwhile a tanker was left burning off Oman, and a US approach to Tehran pulled crude back.

On the markets. Brent traded at one hundred and fourteen dollars ten on Thursday, according to OilPrice dot com. West Texas Intermediate was at one hundred and one dollars fifty, and both were down from Wednesday. European gas touched one hundred euros a megawatt hour on Wednesday, and EU gas storage stood at seventy-four point nine percent full on the twenty-eighth of October, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe.

Europe's stock release comes from France's economy minister, Roland Lescure. He says the forty-two million barrels will go out over three months, according to OilPrice dot com and Rigzone. That works out to roughly four hundred and sixty thousand barrels a day.

France's own share includes ten million barrels of diesel. Lescure says it will start reaching the market within days and will be sold at cost. He expects pump prices to fall by twelve to eighteen cents a litre, possibly from next week.

These figures come from Paris alone. No other government has confirmed them, and neither has the International Energy Agency. It also isn't clear whether the barrels come out of the agency's final one hundred million barrel tranche. Country shares beyond France's haven't been published yet.

The diesel targets Europe's tightest barrel. On Wednesday, Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen called two decades of EU refinery closures a mistake, with diesel at all-time highs, and energy ministers met on Thursday to discuss winter prices. For importers, stock diesel now competes head-on with incoming cargoes. US Gulf Coast barrels have already been priced out of Europe by freight, and diesel cracks in northwest Europe could soften as the French supply lands.
