Qatar's prime minister is taking an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to the White House, and Brent fell about seven percent on Monday. That came on the same day Saudi Arabia's main pipeline around the strait was attacked.

On the markets. Brent fell to ninety-four dollars a barrel by early Monday, and WTI to eighty-eight dollars ten, according to OilPrice dot com. By the close, Brent's loss on the day had reached about seven percent, after a sixteen percent slide last week. European gas storage was seventy-two point four percent full as of Sunday, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe, and US distillate stocks stood at about ninety-nine point three million barrels in the week to the thirtieth of October, according to the EIA.

Starting with the diplomacy. The Qatari prime minister was heading to Washington with Iranian terms for an interim deal to reopen the strait, OilPrice dot com reported. Two diplomatic sources told Israeli media that Tehran has signed off on those terms. Separately, Al Arabiya cited unnamed sources saying Washington would let Iran enrich uranium to just under three point seven percent, under a mediation track run by Qatar and Pakistan.

None of this is confirmed. The accounts are one-sided or unsourced, and neither government has confirmed any terms. President Trump said only that further strikes depend on a very strong agreement, which signals no new attacks for now. Secretary of State Rubio called Tehran's demands so far unacceptable, Rigzone reported.

Even so, crude traders are pricing the Hormuz risk premium out of the market before anything changes physically. And flows are still tight. According to the EIA, US imports of Iraqi crude are at their lowest since the two thousand and three war, falling to zero in several weeks over the summer. Iraq's foreign minister puts the country's war losses at about sixty billion dollars, according to Al Jazeera.

To Saudi Arabia, where the East-West pipeline was attacked in the Medina region on Monday. An Energy Ministry official said the attack caused a limited fire, which was put out, with no casualties and no disruption to operations. The line feeds Yanbu on the Red Sea, the port that handled most of the kingdom's export growth in October.

If the official's account holds, the route around Hormuz is still working. But the attack puts that main bypass directly in the line of fire. Separately, a Houthi attack damaged an Aramco tower in Riyadh's financial district, Rigzone reported. And a Reuters survey put OPEC output back at ninety percent of its pre-war rate.

In the strait itself, tankers were set ablaze over the weekend, and the US military bombed a bulk carrier on Saturday, according to Splash twenty-four seven. Iran issued fresh mine warnings, and explosions were reported along its southern coast. Apart from the US strike, no one has been named as responsible for any of it.

Turning to diesel. Two tankers carrying Russian diesel, the Talisman and the Leruo, have turned away from their declared US destinations after loading at Primorsk in October, OilPrice dot com reported. A third is in the North Atlantic with no declared discharge port.

The Treasury has authorised Russian diesel transactions under the President's arrangement with Putin. Lawmakers, though, warn that importers could still breach a congressional ban. That legal risk is keeping the barrels from landing, so US distillate buyers get no relief from this route for now.

Over to gas. Liquefied natural gas is trading above thirty dollars per million British thermal units going into winter, and Europe holds its lowest gas inventory of the twenty-twenties, according to OilPrice dot com.

China is pulling back from spot buying, as cheap domestic supply and inventory releases kick in. Bloomberg reported China's national average price at about twenty-two dollars, almost ten dollars below the Japan Korea Marker. Weaker Chinese bidding could leave more spot cargoes for Europe.

The catch is demand. Cloudy weather pushed European peak power prices to their highest since twenty twenty-two on Monday, which lifted gas burn. Low wind is expected to last until at least midweek, so gas-fired generators and the utilities buying their power are carrying the cost.
