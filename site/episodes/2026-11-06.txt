Over to US refining. According to the EIA, gasoline made up less than forty-five percent of US refinery output from January through August. That was below the previous five-year low in every one of those months. The usual range runs from forty-five to fifty percent.

The EIA puts the shift down to unusually strong margins on distillates and jet fuel, using monthly supply data through August. Refiners are running hard while they make the switch. Utilisation was ninety-four point five percent in the week to the thirtieth of October, up nearly two points.

Distillate stocks that week stood at about ninety-nine point three million barrels. More middle distillates from Gulf Coast plants eases the US diesel balance, and leaves gasoline relatively short.

Diesel is tightening on the other side of the Pacific. Chinese refiners have sold about five hundred thousand tonnes of diesel for November loading so far, Reuters reported.
